This page catalogues the penalties a UK Self Assessment taxpayer can incur: late filing, late payment, failure to notify chargeability, inaccuracies in returns, and penalties arising from compliance-check obligations. It sets out the statutory tiers and percentages, the reductions available for disclosure, and the three escape routes — reasonable excuse, suspension and special reduction — with what the sources say each does *not* cover. It is an independent summary of primary legislation, HMRC internal manuals and tribunal decisions; it is not an HMRC publication and carries no official status.

## Late filing — FA 2009 Sch 55

Schedule 55 applies to the s.8 TMA 1970 personal return, the s.8A trustee return and the s.122AA partnership return (CH61180). For Income Tax and CGT it first applied to returns for the year ended 5 April 2011 (CH61120); earlier years fall under TMA 1970 s.93 (SALF208).

The filing date is 31 October for paper and 31 January for electronic returns (CH62100). The **penalty date** is the day after the filing date (CH61160, FA09/SCH55/PARA1). Income Tax and CGT sit in what HMRC calls "penalty model 1" (CH62020).

| Stage | Trigger | Amount | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial fixed | Return not filed by filing date | £100 | FA09/SCH55/PARA3; CH62100 |
| Daily | Still outstanding 3 months after penalty date | £10/day, max 90 days | FA09/SCH55/PARA4; CH62120 |
| 6-month | Still outstanding 6 months after penalty date | Greater of 5% of the liability that would have been shown, and £300 | FA09/SCH55/PARA5; CH62140 |
| 12-month — not deliberate | Still outstanding 12 months | Greater of 5% and £300 | FA09/SCH55/PARA6; CH62200 |
| 12-month — deliberate, not concealed | As above, information deliberately withheld | Greater of 70% and £300 | FA09/SCH55/PARA6; CH62240 |
| 12-month — deliberate and concealed | As above, plus arrangements to conceal | Greater of 100% and £300 | FA09/SCH55/PARA6; CH62240 |

Key mechanics:

- **Daily penalties need notice.** HMRC must give written notice specifying the start date, which must be at least 3 months after the penalty date (CH62120).
- **"Liability that would have been shown"** is the net liability after tax deducted at source, and is *not* reduced by amounts already paid towards it (CH63560).
- Where the return is still unfiled, HMRC's practice is to estimate the liability to the best of information and belief and amend the penalty once the true figure is known (CH63560; FA09/SCH55/PARA24). Once assessed, the behaviour percentage can be increased if worse behaviour is later evidenced, but not reduced (CH64250).
- **Only the 70% and 100% tiers can be reduced for disclosure.** The 6-month 5% penalty and the 12-month non-deliberate 5% penalty cannot (CH63120; CH62200).
- **Cap.** Combined tax-geared late-filing penalties must not exceed 100% of the tax liability (FA09/SCH55/PARA17; CH65040, CH404475). HMRC's internal instruction is to adjust in NPPS where the figure exceeds 95% after deducting the automatic 5% (CH403322).
- **Withdrawal of the notice to file.** If HMRC agrees to withdraw the obligation to file, late-filing penalties already charged are cancelled (FA09/SCH55/PARA17A; CH61700, CH64280). Per CH61700 this applies from 2012-13 for individuals and trustees, and for relevant periods beginning on or after 6 April 2012 for partnerships including companies.
- **Partnerships.** A penalty for a late partnership return is payable by every partner, but only the representative partner (or successor) may appeal, and that appeal covers all partners (FA09/SCH55/PARA20, PARA25; CH64540).
- **Double jeopardy.** No filing penalty may be charged where the person has been convicted of a criminal offence in respect of the same failure (FA09/SCH55/PARA26; CH65100).
- **No suspension.** Late-filing penalties cannot be suspended (CH61180).

### Offshore matters (12-month penalty only)

Where withheld information involves an offshore matter and the tax is Income Tax or CGT, the 12-month percentages increase by territory category (FA09/SCH55/PARA6A; CH112600). Territories are categorised by Treasury Order: category 1 = automatic exchange of information with the UK; category 3 = no exchange; anything not listed in 1 or 3 defaults to category 2 (CH403145, CH112400). CH112400 warns that different category tables apply depending on whether the filing date fell before or on/after 24 July 2013, and that the category must be tested at the date of the failure, not today.

| Behaviour | Cat 1 | Cat 2 | Cat 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deliberate and concealed | 100% | 150% | 200% |
| Deliberate, not concealed | 70% | 105% | 140% |
| Any other case | 5% | 5% | 5% |

### Pre-2010-11 returns (TMA 1970 s.93)

For 2009-10 and earlier: £100 fixed penalty (s.93(2)), a further £100 if still outstanding six months after the filing date (s.93(4)), daily penalties by tribunal direction (s.93(3)) and an over-12-month tax-geared penalty (s.93(5)) (SALF208; EM4570). The fixed penalties could not exceed the tax outstanding at the filing date (s.93(7)). Following *Steeden v Carver* (SpC 212), HMRC accepted a reasonable excuse for filing up to midnight on 31 January, and as an administrative practice charged the £100 only where the return was not received by the end of 1 February — the return remained "late" for other purposes such as enquiry windows (SALF208; EM4570).

## Late payment — FA 2009 Sch 56

Schedule 56 late payment penalties replaced TMA 1970 s.59C surcharges; CH404300 records that s.59C surcharges have not been charged for years after 2009-10, and that Sch 56 applies to balancing payments due on or after 31 January 2012.

What the supplied sources establish:

| Point | Rule | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Default penalty as a percentage of tax unpaid at the due date, with further penalties if the failure continues beyond 6 and 12 months (5 and 11 months for excise, SDIL, PPT) | CH150500 |
| Time to pay | A deferral agreed **before** the due date suspends penalties for the deferral period; breaking it makes the person liable as if the agreement never existed, from the date of HMRC's notice | FA09/SCH56/PARA10; CH156600 |
| Assessment time limit | Later of (a) 2 years from the day before the penalty date, and (b) 12 months from the end of the appeal period for the tax assessment (or the date liability is established) | FA09/SCH56/PARA12 |
| Payment | Within 30 days of issue of the penalty assessment; enforceable as if a tax assessment | FA09/SCH56/PARA11(2)&(3) |
| Interaction | Sch 56 penalties apply independently and must **not** be withdrawn in the way surcharges were cancelled | CH404300 |
| Future | Sch 26 FA 2021 late payment penalties are to apply to ITSA from April 2026 | CH150500; CH193000 |

Reasonable excuse and special reduction are both available (CH150550; CH170200). The specific ITSA percentage tiers are not given in the supplied notes — see *Gaps*.

## Failure to notify — FA 2008 Sch 41

For Income Tax, CGT and Class 4 NIC (and Class 2 NIC from 6 April 2015) the relevant obligation is the TMA 1970 s.7 duty to give notice of liability (CH71300; earlier Class 2 years fell under regs 87A–87G of the Social Security (Contributions) Regulations 2001, NIM23008). The deadline is 5 October following the end of the tax year (CH71280). The failure occurs the day after the final date for compliance (CH71240). Sch 41 applies to failures on or after 1 April 2010; for Income Tax/CGT the first period covered is 2009-10 (CH65040; CH401310).

Penalties are a percentage of **potential lost revenue** (PLR) — the tax unpaid because of the failure (CH72620). Maximums by behaviour: deliberate and concealed 100%, deliberate not concealed 70%, non-deliberate 30% (CH72540).

Ranges after reduction for disclosure, for onshore matters (and category 1 offshore up to 2015-16) (CH73200):

| Behaviour | Unprompted | Prompted |
|---|---|---|
| Non-deliberate, HMRC aware within 12 months of tax first becoming unpaid | 0% – 30% | 10% – 30% |
| Non-deliberate, HMRC aware more than 12 months after | 10% – 30% | 20% – 30% |
| Deliberate, not concealed | 20% – 70% | 35% – 70% |
| Deliberate and concealed | 30% – 100% | 50% – 100% |

Behaviour definitions (CH72120, CH72160, CH72200): *deliberate and concealed* = knows of the obligation, is able to comply, does not, and takes active steps to conceal; *deliberate not concealed* = knows and is able but simply does not; *non-deliberate* = neither of the above. No penalty arises for a non-deliberate failure where there is a reasonable excuse and the person notified without unreasonable delay after it ended (FA08/SCH41/PARA20; CH71160). Higher percentages apply to offshore matters and offshore transfers where the tax is Income Tax or CGT (FA08/SCH41/PARA6A, PARA6AA; CH114100).

## Inaccuracies — FA 2007 Sch 24

Three distinct charges (CH81001):

| Provision | Trigger | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Para 1 | Giving HMRC an inaccurate return or document that understates liability, overstates a loss, or overstates a repayment claim, where the inaccuracy is careless or deliberate | FA07/SCH24/PARA1 |
| Para 1A | Another person (T) deliberately supplies false information to, or withholds information from, P, intending P's document to be inaccurate — chargeable on T whether or not P is also penalised | FA07/SCH24/PARA1A |
| Para 2 | Failure to take reasonable steps to notify HMRC of an under-assessment within 30 days of the assessment | FA07/SCH24/PARA2; CH81170 |

Commencement for Income Tax, CGT, PAYE, NICs, CIS and VAT: paras 1 and 2 apply where the document was due to be filed on or after 1 April 2009 and relates to a tax period beginning on or after 1 April 2008; para 1A from filing dates on or after 1 April 2010 and periods beginning on or after 1 April 2009 (CH81012). TMA 1970 ss.100–103 do not apply to Sch 24 penalties (CH81001).

Maximum penalties as a percentage of PLR (CH82120):

| Behaviour | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Mistake despite reasonable care | No penalty (CH81130) |
| Careless (failure to take reasonable care) | 30% |
| Deliberate, not concealed | 70% |
| Deliberate and concealed | 100% |
| Inaccuracy attributable to another person's deliberate behaviour | 100% |
| Failure to notify an under-assessment | 30% |

Ranges after disclosure reduction (FA07/SCH24/PARA10; CH82470, CH82510):

| Behaviour | Unprompted | Prompted |
|---|---|---|
| Careless / under-assessment | 0% – 30% | 15% – 30% |
| Deliberate, not concealed | 20% – 70% | 35% – 70% |
| Deliberate and concealed | 30% – 100% | 50% – 100% |
| Attributable to another person | 30% – 100% | 50% – 100% |

Points on behaviour:

- **Careless** is a failure to take reasonable care, judged, per the FTT in *David Collis* as summarised at CH81140, by the standard of a prudent and reasonable person in that person's position.
- **Deliberate** requires only that the person knew the figure given was wrong; it is not necessary that they knew the correct figure (CH81150).
- **Concealment** means active steps to cover tracks — false invoices, backdated documents, destroyed records, diverted takings (CH402706, CH81160).
- **Agents do not transfer responsibility.** HMRC's view is that a person who goes to an apparently competent adviser, gives full and accurate facts, checks the work to the best of their ability and adopts it has taken reasonable care; otherwise the person remains liable and bears the onus of showing reasonable care (CH84540; FA07/SCH24/PARA18).
- **Avoidance arrangements.** For documents submitted on or after 16 November 2017 relating to a tax period beginning on or after 6 April 2017 and ending after 15 November 2017, an inaccuracy arising from avoidance arrangements is *presumed careless* unless HMRC shows it was deliberate or the person shows reasonable care (FA07/SCH24/PARA3A; CH81122). Reliance on "disqualified advice" — from an interested person, an adviser lacking appropriate expertise, or advice ignoring the person's individual circumstances — cannot establish reasonable care (FA07/SCH24/PARA3B; CH81123).
- **Partnerships.** Each partner whose tax is affected by an inaccuracy in the partnership return incurs their own "partner's penalty" on their own PLR; only the nominated partner may appeal, and that appeal covers all (FA07/SCH24/PARA20; CH84730, CH84760).
- **Later discovery.** An inaccuracy that was neither careless nor deliberate when made is treated as careless if the person discovers it and fails to take reasonable steps to tell HMRC (FA07/SCH24/PARA3(2); CH81080).
- **Double jeopardy.** No civil penalty for the same behaviour where the person has been convicted of a criminal offence (FA07/SCH24/PARA21; CH84900).

## Reductions for disclosure

The same architecture runs through Sch 24, Sch 41 and Sch 55. HMRC's manuals describe it as two steps (CH403201, CH82510):

1. **Prompted or unprompted?** A disclosure is *unprompted* if made at a time when the person had no reason to believe HMRC had discovered, or was about to discover, the inaccuracy, failure or withheld information; anything else is prompted (FA07/SCH24/PARA9(2); CH82420, CH63140). Once a compliance check into specific returns has started, disclosure about those items cannot be unprompted, though undisclosed items outside the check may still qualify (CH82421). Disclosing because you believe HMRC will find out anyway does not make it unprompted (CH403145).
2. **Quality of disclosure**, scored across three elements totalling 100% of the available range (CH403203, CH63220, CH73240):

| Element | Weight | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Telling | 30% | Admitting it, disclosing in full, explaining how and why (CH63260, CH73300) |
| Helping | 40% | Active assistance in quantifying the tax — not passive acceptance (CH73320) |
| Giving access | 30% | Responding positively to requests for records and documents (CH73340, CH63300) |

The resulting percentage reduces the gap between the maximum and minimum for the behaviour/disclosure combination. HMRC's formula (CH82510): penalty percentage = maximum − (disclosure quality % × (maximum − minimum)); penalty = PLR × that percentage.

**Timing restriction.** For disclosures or corrections made after 5 September 2016, where a "significant period" — normally over three years, measured from when the inaccuracy first occurred or from the filing date — elapsed before the person began telling HMRC, the maximum reduction is cut by 10 percentage points *before* telling/helping/giving are scored (CH403204, CH82465, CH63310, CH73360). HMRC officers may disapply it but need manager or authorising-officer sign-off and recorded reasons (CH73360, CH63310). The same restriction is applied where an offshore disclosure facility was previously available and not used (CH82465).

**Offshore additional information.** For offshore matters and offshore transfers in 2016-17 and later, a fourth element — information about enablers and about offshore assets/nominees — must be provided or the reduction is not fully available (Sch 21 FA 2016; CH63220, CH117000, CH403203).

**Publication.** Where a compliance check produces qualifying PLR (the part attributable to deliberate behaviour) above £25,000, HMRC may publish the person's details as a deliberate defaulter; qualifying PLR from separate investigations cannot be aggregated to reach the threshold (CH190320; worked examples at CH203xxx). Deliberate behaviour may also lead to inclusion in the Managing Serious Defaulters programme (CH480000).

## Suspension

Suspension exists only under FA 2007 Sch 24 para 14 and only for **careless inaccuracies**. HMRC's manuals treat the following as an absolute bar with no exceptions (CH83132, CH83142):

- deliberate inaccuracies (concealed or not);
- inaccuracies attributable to another person (para 1A);
- failure to take reasonable steps to notify an under-assessment (para 2).

Late-filing penalties (Sch 55) cannot be suspended at all (CH61180).

| Feature | Rule | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum period | 2 years from the date of the notice to suspend | FA07/SCH24/PARA14(2)(b); CH83170 |
| Conditions | At least one **specific** SMART condition (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound) addressing the cause, **plus** the generic condition to file all returns on time during the period | FA07/SCH24/PARA14(3)&(4); CH83153–CH83155 |
| Generic payment condition | Removed for penalties suspended from 8 April 2013 | CH83155 |
| If no SMART condition can be set | Suspension is not possible even for careless behaviour (e.g. a one-off error, or a taxpayer retiring from SA) | CH83143; CH404300 |
| Trigger to collect | A further para 1 penalty during the period, or failure to satisfy HMRC that conditions were met at the end | FA07/SCH24/PARA14(5)&(6); CH83200 |
| Appeal | Against the decision not to suspend, partial suspension, or the conditions set — **not** against the decision to collect a suspended penalty (judicial review only) | CH83158, CH83190, CH404300 |
| Tribunal power | Can order suspension only if HMRC's decision not to suspend was "flawed" on judicial review principles | CH83120 |
| Interest | Late payment interest does not run while a Sch 24 penalty is suspended | CH140240 |

The penalty must still be assessed (or included in a contract settlement) even where suspension is given (FA07/SCH24/PARA14; CH83110), and HMRC's instruction is that suspension must be decided separately from, and not traded against, the penalty amount or an appeal (CH83182).

## Special reduction

Special reduction is HMRC's discretion to reduce a penalty — including below the statutory minimum — where **special circumstances** exist. It applies to penalties under FA07/Sch 24, FA08/Sch 41, FA09/Sch 55, FA09/Sch 56, FA16/Sch 22, FA21/Sch 25, FA21/Sch 26 and FA22/Sch 14 (CH170200). It includes staying a penalty (stopping or postponing enforcement) and agreeing a compromise (CH170500).

**What counts.** CH170600 defines special circumstances as factors not otherwise provided for in penalty legislation, that are either relevant and sufficiently special that it is right to reduce the penalty, or where strict application produces a result contrary to the clear compliance intention of the law. Per the Upper Tribunal in *Barry Edwards*, as summarised at CH173000, the term is not to be given a restrictive interpretation and can include any relevant factor, whether or not specific to the individual. The UT in *Matthew Harrison* added that glosses such as "uncommon or exceptional" neither add to nor detract from the statutory word "special".

**What the sources say it is not** (CH170900):

- ability to pay;
- balancing one taxpayer's underpayment against another's overpayment;
- anything already accounted for elsewhere in the penalty scheme — reasonable excuse, reasonable care, quality-of-disclosure reductions;
- the size of the penalty alone (worked example: £100 fixed plus £600 daily on a £200 liability was not reduced);
- proportionality as a free-standing argument — HMRC's stated position is that the regimes are ECHR-compliant.
- Where behaviour is deliberate, HMRC says special reduction is unlikely to apply.

**Process.** HMRC officers must not give a special reduction without authority from the Specialist Technical Team and must not discuss the amount with the taxpayer (CH170100, CH403265). They are instructed to consider it before issuing any non-automated penalty assessment, and in every appeal against an automated penalty whether or not it is requested (CH174000, CH403270). CH170600 also records an SA-specific instruction: where a person did not meet SA criteria, late-filing penalties should be cancelled under operational guidance — regardless of whether HMRC failed to act on information that a return should not have been issued.

**On appeal**, the tribunal may substitute its own decision only if HMRC's special-reduction decision was "flawed" applying judicial review principles (FA09/SCH55/PARA22(4); CH64600).

## Reasonable excuse

Reasonable excuse is a defence to late filing, late payment, failure to notify and information-notice penalties. It is not a defence to an inaccuracy penalty under Sch 24 (there the equivalent is reasonable care) (CH170400), and HMRC's position is that it does not apply once behaviour is established as deliberate (CH402252, CH93300).

There is **no statutory definition** of "reasonable excuse" or "unreasonable delay" (CH160100, CH26340). HMRC treats it as something that stops a person meeting an obligation despite having taken reasonable care, judged objectively against what a reasonable person with that taxpayer's attributes and experience would have done — the *Clean Car Company* test (LON/90/138X), with *Rowland* [2006] STC (SCD) 536 confirming it depends on all the circumstances (CH160100, CH26340). The excuse must exist on or before the date of the obligation and throughout the period of default, and the failure must be put right without unreasonable delay once it ends (CH160100, CH160200).

The Upper Tribunal in *Perrin* [2018] UKUT 156 (TC), para 81, set the four-stage test HMRC's manual adopts:

1. establish the facts asserted to give rise to the excuse;
2. decide which of those facts are proven, on the balance of probabilities;
3. decide objectively whether the proven facts amount to a reasonable excuse, and when it ceased;
4. decide whether the failure was remedied without unreasonable delay after it ceased.

The UT in *Marlow Rowing Club* [2020] UKUT 20 (TCC) cautioned against over-reliance on other FTT reasonable excuse decisions, as each turns on its own facts (CH170400).

| May amount to a reasonable excuse (illustrative only) | Ref |
|---|---|
| Bereavement; serious physical illness; mental health condition affecting ability to comply | CH160300, CH160400, CH160500 |
| Loss of records through fire, flood, theft or computer failure | CH160300, CH26340 |
| HMRC Online Service failure (error message and date required); activation PIN delayed where registration was before the filing date; replacement credentials applied for **before** the deadline | CH — Service issues with HMRC Online Service |
| Postal disruption | CH160300, CH26340 |
| Ignorance of, or misunderstanding of, a legal obligation — *if* objectively reasonable for that taxpayer | CH160600; *Perrin* |
| Insufficiency of funds **attributable to events outside the person's control**, despite reasonable foresight and due diligence | CH — Insufficiency of funds |

| Not a reasonable excuse | Ref |
|---|---|
| Insufficiency of funds in itself | CH160800; CH26420 |
| Reliance on another person in itself, unless the person took reasonable care — explaining requirements, setting deadlines, checking progress | CH160700, CH26440 |
| An agent's default where the agent acts as administrator/functionary rather than professional adviser | *Lithgow* [2012] UKFTT 620 (TC), summarised at CH160700 |
| Cash-flow problems, or anticipating inability to pay, as an excuse for not **filing** | CH — Insufficiency of funds |
| Agent's failure to act in time; complexity of affairs; being too busy; non-liability or overpayment of tax (in the late-appeal context) | ARTG2215 |
| An excuse arising **after** the date of the obligation | CH160100 |

The onus of establishing reasonable excuse is on the taxpayer (CH72240, CH26380). ARTG2215 also records that the amount of tax involved is not relevant to whether a late-appeal excuse is reasonable, and that the tribunal — unlike an HMRC decision maker — is not confined to the reasonable excuse conditions and may admit a late appeal in the interests of justice.

## Standard and burden of proof

HMRC must establish, on the **balance of probabilities**, that a failure occurred and whether it was careless, deliberate, or deliberate and concealed (CH72260, CH81190, CH93400). The manual notes that although penalties are treated as "criminal" for Article 6 ECHR purposes, the civil standard applies (CH301000), but that the *quality* of evidence should be higher for more serious behaviour (CH — Level of proof), and CH401310 records that tribunals require stronger evidence to displace an inherent presumption of honesty. The taxpayer bears the burden on reasonable excuse (CH72240) and, for avoidance-related inaccuracies, on reasonable care (CH81122).

## Record keeping and compliance-check penalties

**Record keeping.** TMA 1970 s.12B requires records sufficient to make a correct and complete return. CH10100 states that record-keeping requirements were aligned across taxes from 1 April 2009 for income tax, CGT, corporation tax, VAT and direct tax claims not made in a return, and that HMRC may charge a penalty for failure to keep or retain records, with no change to existing penalty regimes. The supplied notes do not give the amount — see *Gaps*.

**Information notices and inspections (FA 2008 Sch 36).** These are not SA filing penalties but routinely arise in SA enquiries. TMA 1970 ss.100–103 do not apply to them (CH25600).

| Trigger | Penalty | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Failure to comply with an information notice; deliberate obstruction of a tribunal-approved inspection; concealing or destroying required documents | £300 initial | FA08/SCH36/PARA39; CH26640 |
| Continuing failure after the initial penalty is assessed | Up to £60/day | FA08/SCH36/PARA40; CH26660 |
| Continuing failure, identity-unknown notices (only after daily penalties charged) | Up to £1,000/day | CH26705 |
| Inaccurate information or documents supplied in response to a notice | Up to £3,000 per inaccuracy | FA08/SCH36/PARA40A; CH26780 |
| Concealing/destroying documents after informal notification, or after a tribunal-approved notice | Criminal offence | FA08/SCH36/PARA53, PARA54; CH27200 |

A daily or increased-daily penalty cannot be charged unless an initial penalty has first been assessed (CH26620). Reasonable excuse is available (FA08/SCH36/PARA45).

**Falsifying documents.** Intentionally falsifying, concealing, destroying or disposing of a document required under a TMA 1970 s.20BA order is an offence: on summary conviction a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum; on indictment up to two years' imprisonment, a fine, or both (TMA 1970 s.20BB(5)).

## How penalties interact

| Situation | Required adjustment | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Inaccuracy/under-assessment penalty plus the automatic 5% tax-geared failure-to-file penalty (IT, CGT, CIS) | Reduce the inaccuracy penalty by the 5% penalty already charged | CH404450 |
| 6-month plus 12-month tax-geared failure-to-file penalties exceeding 100% of the liability | Adjust so the combined total does not exceed 100% (not increased for category 1 offshore) | FA09/SCH55/PARA17; CH404475, CH63620 |
| Multiple penalties on the same tax liability (failure to notify, late filing, inaccuracy) | Reduce one or more so the combined percentage does not exceed the highest single applicable percentage; cannot reduce a failure-to-file penalty below zero; does not apply between two failure-to-file penalties or against a Sch 56 late payment penalty | CH65060, CH84970 |
| Para 1A penalties on both P and T for the same inaccuracy | Reduce each proportionately so the aggregate does not exceed 100% of PLR (with limited offshore exceptions) | FA07/SCH24/PARA12(4)&(5); CH84974, CH404500 |
| Failure-to-notify penalty plus a s.59C surcharge on the same liability | Discharge the surcharge | CH404425 |
| Sch 24 penalty and a TMA 1970 s.98 penalty on the same document | Mutually exclusive — no Sch 24 penalty is payable | FA07/SCH24/PARA12(1); CH84960 |

## Assessment, payment and appeals

| Penalty | Assessment time limit | Payment |
|---|---|---|
| Late filing (Sch 55) | Later of 2 years from the filing date, and 12 months from the end of the appeal period for the tax assessment (or the date liability is ascertained) | 30 days from issue (PARA18(2)&(3)) |
| Late payment (Sch 56) | Later of 2 years from the day before the penalty date, and 12 months from the end of the appeal period | 30 days from issue (PARA11(2)&(3)) |
| Failure to notify (Sch 41) | Assessment must state the date, legislation, PLR, HMRC's view of behaviour, disclosure reduction, penalty amount and relevant period | Per notice (PARA16; CH74150) |

An assessment must set out the date of assessment, the amount, the legislation relied on, the tax period and appeal rights, with a separate entry per inaccurate document and per period (CH64150, CH83020, CH83030). Penalty assessments are enforceable as if they were tax assessments (FA09/SCH55/PARA18; FA09/SCH56/PARA11).

Appeals follow the standard route (ARTG2215, ARTG2220): 30 days to appeal a decision notice; HMRC must set out its "view of the matter" before a review can be offered or begin (TMA 1970 ss.49B(2), 49C(2)); the customer then has 30 days to accept the review or notify the tribunal; the statutory review period is 45 days from the "view of the matter" letter. A late appeal must be accepted where the person had a reasonable excuse and appealed without unreasonable delay after it ceased (TMA 1970 s.49(3)). The penalty need not be paid before an appeal is considered (CH64500+).

## Machine-implementable rules

Stated in input/condition/result form, for the Income Tax/CGT SA return under Sch 55:

```
inputs: filing_method, filing_date, actual_filing_date, liability, behaviour,
        daily_notice_given, daily_start_date, reasonable_excuse, notice_withdrawn

filing_date      = 31 Oct (paper) | 31 Jan (electronic)          [CH62100]
penalty_date     = filing_date + 1 day                            [CH61160]

if notice_withdrawn:            all late-filing penalties = 0     [PARA17A]
if reasonable_excuse AND remedied_without_unreasonable_delay: 0   [CH160100]

p_initial  = 100                              if not filed by filing_date       [PARA3]
p_daily    = 10 * min(days_since(daily_start_date), 90)
             if still unfiled 3 months after penalty_date AND daily_notice_given [PARA4]
p_6month   = max(0.05 * liability, 300)       if unfiled at penalty_date + 6m   [PARA5]

rate_12m   = 1.00 if behaviour == deliberate_and_concealed
           | 0.70 if behaviour == deliberate_not_concealed
           | 0.05 otherwise                                                     [PARA6]
p_12month  = max(rate_12m * liability, 300)   if unfiled at penalty_date + 12m

# disclosure reduction applies only to the 70% / 100% tiers      [CH63120]
if rate_12m in (0.70, 1.00):
    range   = (max_pct, min_pct) from CH63200 by behaviour x prompted/unprompted
    if significant_period_over_3_years and disclosure_after_2016-09-05:
        max_reduction = (max_pct - min_pct) - 0.10                [CH63310]
    pct     = max_pct - (quality_score * max_reduction)
    p_12month = max(pct * liability, 300)

# statutory cap on tax-geared penalties
assert (p_6month + p_12month) <= 1.00 * liability                 [PARA17]
```

Sch 24 penalty amount (CH82510): `penalty = PLR × (max_pct − (quality_score × (max_pct − min_pct)))`, where `quality_score = telling(≤0.30) + helping(≤0.40) + giving(≤0.30)`.

Suspension eligibility: `suspendable = (regime == FA07_Sch24) AND (behaviour == careless) AND NOT attributable_to_another_person AND NOT under_assessment_para2 AND at_least_one_SMART_condition_available` (CH83132, CH83143).

## Gaps in the sources

The supplied notes do not cover, and this page therefore does not state:

- **The ITSA late payment penalty percentages and their trigger dates.** CH150500 confirms only that Sch 56 charges a percentage of tax unpaid at the due date with further penalties beyond 6 and 12 months; the specific ITSA rates and the 30-day / 5-month structure are not in the notes. CH156650 shows a 5% figure but in a PAYE deferral example.
- **The amount of any record-keeping penalty** under TMA 1970 s.12B. CH10100 confirms only that "HMRC may charge a penalty" for failure to keep or retain records. The £3,000 figure at TMA 1970 s.98(2) appears in the notes only as a generic illustration of a "maximum-amount penalty" (EM4506), not tied to record keeping.
- **The specific retention periods** for SA records.
- **Sch 26 FA 2021 points-based late submission penalties and the new late payment regime**, beyond the note that Sch 26 applies to ITSA from April 2026 (CH150500).
- **Failure to Correct (FTC) penalty amounts** under Sch 18 F(No.2)A 2017. The notes give the 30 September 2018 correction deadline and the disclosure-channel exceptions (CH123260) but not the penalty percentages.
- **The full Sch 55 minimum-percentage table for offshore categories 2 and 3 at 2016-17 and later** is given at CH112700 for prompted/unprompted deliberate behaviour but is not reproduced exhaustively here for every combination.
- Several cited manual pages are recorded in the notes as **withheld under Freedom of Information Act 2000 exemptions** (e.g. parts of CH301100, CH403140, CH124660, CH883610), or as archived (CH171000) or under review (CH402336, CH883600), so HMRC's full internal position on those points is not visible.
