This page covers two linked topics: the records a Self Assessment taxpayer is required to keep and preserve, and the powers HMRC has to demand information, inspect premises and penalise non-compliance. Record-keeping duties sit mainly in TMA 1970 s.12B; the information and inspection powers sit in Schedule 36 FA 2008, with adjacent regimes for bulk third-party data (Sch 23 FA 2011) and dishonest tax agents (Sch 38 FA 2012). Most of the operational detail below comes from HMRC's Compliance Handbook and Enquiry Manual, which are HMRC's own interpretation and internal instruction, not law.

## 1. What records must be kept

### 1.1 The core duty

A person required to make a Self Assessment return must keep the records needed to make and deliver a correct and complete return for the tax year or period — and the duty applies even if a return is not made every year (TMA 1970 s.12B; CH11200; EM1450). HMRC's stated standard is that records be kept up to date and in sufficient detail to (a) make a correct and complete return, (b) calculate the correct tax due, and (c) allow HMRC to check the figures (CH10200).

Additional requirements apply to those carrying on a trade, profession or business, alone or in partnership: records of all receipts and expenses, records of matters relating to those receipts and expenditure, and — where dealing in goods — records of all sales and purchases of goods (TMA 1970 s.12B; CH11300). For these purposes **"trade" includes the letting of property** (CH11300; CH14510; CH14530).

A person making a direct tax claim *outside* a return must keep all records needed to make a correct and complete claim (TMA 1970 Sch 1A para 2A; CH11500).

HMRC does not prescribe in detail what records must be kept beyond the general and additional requirements above (CH note on CH11200–CH11400). From 1 April 2009 HMRC has power to make regulations specifying records required or not required for income tax, CGT, corporation tax and claims not in a return, but the Compliance Handbook states no such regulations have been made (CH11100; CH10100; FA 2008 Sch 37).

### 1.2 "Statutory records" — a term that does real work

Schedule 36 defines statutory records as information or a document a person is required to keep and preserve by the Taxes Acts or other relevant legislation; they cease to be statutory records when the preservation period expires (FA 2008 Sch 36 para 62; CH21700). This matters because **there is no right of appeal against a requirement in an information notice to produce statutory records** (CH24320; Sch 36 paras 29–31), and because inspection powers reach only business documents that form part of the statutory records (Sch 36 para 10(3); CH25280).

HMRC's view on timing (CH21700; CH212100):

| Record type | When it becomes a statutory record |
|---|---|
| Business and VAT records required for tax purposes | When created — so current-year records can be required or inspected where justified |
| Non-business records required for tax purposes | Only after the end of the chargeable period — so they cannot be inspected during the year, and not at a residence during the year |

CH212200 / CH212300 further split statutory records into *specified* records (named in legislation, e.g. certain VAT records, Companies Act accounting records) and *non-specified* records (not named, but which must be sufficient to make a complete and accurate return). Records that are **not** statutory records but are reasonably required to check a tax position are called *supplementary* records; a taxpayer notice requiring these **is** appealable (CH212100).

### 1.3 Retention periods

Records must be retained until the **latest** of the applicable dates below (TMA 1970 s.12B; CH14530; CH14550):

| Taxpayer | Baseline retention date | Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Carries on trade, profession or business (incl. property letting) — tax-year return | 5th anniversary of the 31 January next following the year of assessment | and until completion of any enquiry, and until the end of the day the enquiry window closes |
| Carries on trade, profession or business — return for a period that is not a tax year | 6th anniversary of the end of that period | as above |
| Does **not** carry on a trade, profession or business | 1st anniversary of the 31 January next following the year of assessment | as above |
| Claim not included in a return | — | later of completion of an enquiry into the claim, or the day after the enquiry window for the claim closes (TMA 1970 Sch 1A para 2A; CH14900) |

Enquiry window closure, per CH14530/CH14550: **12 months after the date the return was filed** if filed on time; **the first anniversary of the next quarter day falling after** the filing date if filed late.

Edge cases:

- If HMRC gives a notice to file *after* the normal retention date has already passed, any records still held must be kept until the later of enquiry completion or enquiry window closure (CH14530; CH14550).
- Where the same record is needed for more than one tax with different periods, the longer period applies (CH14100).
- Records kept overseas must be made available in the UK when required (CH217000).
- Computer-based records: retain the programs, or make arrangements to reconstruct data, if programs are altered or upgraded, and be able to convert computer-held data into a satisfactory legible form on request (CH15500).
- HMRC can specify a **shorter** retention period in writing, on individual request, but cannot lengthen it; it must never do so where avoidance is suspected, nor for PAYE/CIS records. The Compliance Handbook states HMRC has not specified any shorter periods so far (CH14100; CH15300; CH15500).
- Adjacent regimes for comparison: PAYE and CIS records, 3 years after the end of the tax year (SI 2003/2682 reg 97(1); SI 2005/2045 reg 51(10); CH14700) — with **no penalty** for failure to keep PAYE/NIC or CIS records (CH219100). Companies Act 2006 s.388: 6 years for a plc, 3 years for a private company, though tax law may require longer.

### 1.4 Penalty for failing to keep or preserve records

| Element | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum penalty | £3,000 | TMA 1970 s.12B(5); EM4650 |
| Unit of charge | One failure per return or claim | EM4650 |
| Parallel provisions | Claims outside a return: TMA 1970 Sch 1A para 2A(4). Companies: FA 1998 Sch 18 para 23 | EM4650 |
| Mitigation | May be set below the maximum under TMA 1970 s.100(1) | EM4650 |
| Appeal | Normal appeal rights | EM4650 |

HMRC's *policy* (not law) is that it does not intend to seek s.12B(5)-type penalties in every case of inadequate record-keeping, and reserves them for more serious cases (EM4650, recording internal assurances). EM4650 instructs officers to send a written warning letter (copied to the agent) on the first occasion a failure is established, unless records were deliberately destroyed, and to obtain authorising-officer approval before penalty action for later or serious failures (e.g. deliberate destruction to obstruct an enquiry, or a history of failures). CH219100 adds that officers must act proportionately, and gives an example where a penalty would not be appropriate: inadequate records arising from the sole proprietor's death after illness. Warning letters are not penalty determinations and carry no right of appeal (EM4650).

### 1.5 Records and behaviour-based penalties

Poor records bite indirectly, and often harder than s.12B(5):

- HMRC treats making and preserving sufficient records as part of "reasonable care" for inaccuracy penalties (FA 2007 Sch 24 para 3(1)(a); CH81120). Failure to take reasonable care makes an inaccuracy careless.
- Destroyed records are listed by HMRC as evidence of **concealment**, pushing an inaccuracy into the "deliberate and concealed" band (CH402706; CH81160; FA 2007 Sch 24 para 3(1)(c)).
- CH402336 works through *TC/2012/03906* (Dr Harper), where sparse records plus an implausible unsupported explanation for their absence (an alleged break-in), plus an £18,000 understatement against £21,000 returned profit, supported a finding of deliberate inaccuracy. HMRC's own note stresses the *relative* size matters: the same £18,000 discrepancy against £110,000 returned profit would likely not, on size alone, prove deliberate behaviour.
- Conversely, a careless-inaccuracy penalty may be **suspended** on SMART conditions requiring record-keeping improvements — e.g. retaining invoices and receipts, keeping a manual record of outgoings (FA 2007 Sch 24 para 14(3)&(4); CH405177/CH405179; CH83151). At least one specific SMART condition plus a generic condition that all returns be filed on time during the suspension period must be set (CH83151).
- Giving access to business and other records at a convenient location, and providing copies, is part of the "giving" element of quality-of-disclosure reductions (CH82460; CH63300). Failure to give full access restricts the reduction.

## 2. Information notices (Sch 36 FA 2008)

Pre-31 March 2009 notices under TMA 1970 s.20, s.19A, FA 1998 Sch 18 para 27 and TMA 1970 Sch 1A para 6 can no longer be issued; Sch 36 must be used instead (EM4650 caveat; EM4701–EM4703). Guidance on the old £50 initial penalty under TMA 1970 s.97AA(1)(a) is therefore historic.

### 2.1 Types of notice

| Notice | Purpose | Approval needed | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taxpayer notice | Information/documents from a person to check their own tax position or collect their tax debt | None ordinarily; authorised officer if documents >6 years old, or if applying for tribunal approval | CH221000; CH23080; CH22140 |
| Third party notice | From one person to check the tax position of another **named** person | Agreement of the named taxpayer **or** the tribunal (with exceptions) | Sch 36 paras 2–4; CH23620 |
| Financial Institution Notice (FIN) | From a financial institution about another known person | Designated authorised officer; **no** taxpayer or tribunal approval | CH23105; CH23120; CH21720 |
| Identity unknown notice | To check the tax position of a person/class whose identity is unknown | Tribunal approval (with exceptions); given by an authorised officer | Sch 36 para 5; CH23900; CH24200 |
| Identification notice | Name, address and DOB only, where identity is unknown but ascertainable; **cannot** require documents | Authorised officer; conditions A–D; refer to Specialist Technical Team | Sch 36 para 5A; CH23991; CH23995 |
| Excise information notice | Duties of excise | Separate regime | CEMA 1979 s.118BA; CH228000 |

The **"involved third party"** route (Sch 36 para 34A) existed only for notices given between 1 April 2010 and 31 March 2012 and was replaced from 1 April 2012 by the Sch 23 FA 2011 data-gathering powers (CH23820; CH23980; CH28000).

### 2.2 The Self Assessment-specific restriction

This is the most important SA-specific rule. Once a Self Assessment return has been made for a chargeable period, HMRC may issue a taxpayer notice to check the income tax or CGT position for that period **only** if one of the following holds (FA 2008 Sch 36 para 21; CH23540):

1. there is an **open enquiry** — a notice under TMA 1970 s.9A or s.12AC (or Sch 1A para 5) has been given and no closure notice under s.28A or s.28C (or Sch 1A para 57) has yet been given for the matters to which the request relates; where a **partial** closure notice has been given, the request is restricted to matters not closed by it; or
2. a **potential discovery assessment position** exists — there is reason to suspect that tax may not have been assessed, may have been under-assessed, or that relief given may be excessive, and if the suspicion were true the position could be corrected (TMA 1970 s.29(4)&(5); CH23540); or
3. the notice is needed for checking another tax, or reductions/repayments of tax.

"Reason to suspect" (Sch 36 para 21(6); CH23560) means facts which lead an officer to think the person may have been under-assessed or underpaid, or claimed excessive relief. HMRC's guidance says it is a lower threshold than being able to make an assessment already, but does **not** permit speculative or fishing enquiries — specific risks must be identifiable. CH23540 notes partial closure notices restrict further requests only in enquiries into ITSA or CTSA returns, not SDLT/ATED.

Outside these constraints, a "check" is broad: it "includes carrying out an investigation or enquiry of any kind, ranging from a short telephone call to a full detailed investigation of a person's entire financial affairs over a period of years" (CH21660). HMRC's position is that a check needs no pre-existing risk (random checks are possible) and does not require a return to have been filed — powers can be used against "ghosts" (economically active but unknown to HMRC) and "moonlighters" (known for one source, with another undisclosed) (CH21660; CH21680). Where only extended time limits are available, the officer must have reason to suspect careless or deliberate behaviour (CH21660).

### 2.3 What a notice can require, and how

- **Provide information** means the thing must reach HMRC, not merely be made available for collection; information is knowledge or facts, not opinion or speculation (CH23240). **Produce a document** means bring it out and show it; the document must already exist when the notice is given, and HMRC cannot require a new document to be created to satisfy a "produce" requirement (CH23260; note 22 caveat).
- The duty extends to documents in the person's **possession** (physical control, ownership irrelevant) or **power** (ability to get it, or a copy, from whoever holds it, by legal entitlement or influence) (Sch 36 para 18; CH22120).
- Notices must **specify** identifiable documents as clearly as possible, or **describe** them as clearly and narrowly as possible where specification is impossible (CH23380; CH23400). CH23340 records a Parliamentary assurance that these powers will not be used for speculative enquiries.
- **Period for compliance** must be reasonable (Sch 36 para 7(1)); HMRC's "general rule of thumb" is that most information could be provided within 30 days from the date of the notice, longer around a business's seasonal peaks (CH23420). There is no statutory minimum.
- Time and place must be reasonable; a notice cannot specify a place used solely as a dwelling for document production (CH23170 caveat; CH23280). *Johnson v Blackpool Commissioners and CIR* (70 TC 1) is cited for a penalty upheld where an unreasonable time (11:59pm) was suggested (CH23280).
- HMRC may take copies, extracts, or remove documents; on removal it must give a written receipt if asked and provide a copy free of charge if reasonably required (Sch 36 paras 15 and 16; CH23300).
- A **lien** over someone else's document is not broken by producing it to HMRC (CH23260).
- Service: by delivery to the person, by leaving it at their usual or last known place of residence, or by email **only** with the customer's explicit informed consent specific to that notice (CH23440). For companies, service may be on the proper officer — normally the company secretary (CH223500; CTM00530). A notice is not automatically void for formal defects if it meets the intention of the law and adequately identifies the affected person (TMA 1970 ss.108, 114, 115; CH23440).
- A notice must not include anything that could identify an informant (CH221000).

### 2.4 Restrictions on what can be demanded

| Restriction | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Documents originating **more than 6 years** before the notice | Requires prior agreement of an authorised officer. If not obtained, the person has **not** failed to comply by declining to produce it. Extended to **10 years** for QROPS/former QROPS notices | Sch 36 para 20; CH22140; CH23990 |
| Documents relating to the **conduct of a pending appeal** | Excluded. Means documents brought into existence as part of preparing an appeal — not pre-existing evidence | Sch 36 para 19(1)(a); CH22160 |
| **Personal records** (PACE 1984 s.12 — physical, mental, spiritual or personal welfare of an individual) | Restricted; officer must discuss with Specialist Technical Team before seeking access; only where no other effective way to complete the check; must not be used for any other purpose | Sch 36 paras 19(2)&(3); CH22180; CH22200 |
| **Journalistic material** (PACE 1984 s.13) | Excluded | Sch 36 para 19(1)(b); CH22220 |
| **Auditors' papers** | Protected; "auditor" defined by statutory appointment or equivalent independent audit, provided audit work is kept separate from accounts preparation | Sch 36 para 24; CH22280 |
| **Tax advisers' "relevant communications"** | Communications between adviser and client (or another adviser of the client) for the purpose of giving or obtaining tax advice, belonging to the adviser | Sch 36 para 25(3); CH22300; CH22320 |
| **Legal professional privilege** | Arises principally from common law, backed by DPA 1998, Art 8 HRA and Sch 36 para 23. Covers the client's privilege, not the professional's own | Sch 36 para 23; CH207328 |
| **Medical records** | Records containing medical information (not appointment diaries merely naming a patient); HMRC officers must consult and, if required, obtain approval before issuing any notice for them, and must not use the information for any other purpose | CH207326 |

Two important carve-outs from the adviser/auditor protection. First, protection does not apply to **linking information** — information showing *how* an entry in the accounts or return was arrived at, as distinct from *why* (CH231300). Second, protection under Sch 36 para 26(3) does not apply if the linking information is contained in another document already delivered to or inspected by HMRC (CH231300). HMRC's guidance is that access to accountants' linking documents should not be sought routinely, and formal Sch 36 procedures require prior written managerial approval confirming alternative avenues were explored (CH231300; CH231400).

Where a notice covers part of a document, the unprotected parts must still be produced; protected parts may be redacted or covered up, and HMRC may still require the original (CH23340; CH23480). Client confidentiality alone is not a valid ground to refuse an information notice, though Article 8 considerations may still be relevant (CH22200 caveat).

### 2.5 Third party notices in practice

- HMRC should try to obtain the information from the person whose position is being checked before approaching a third party (CH23620; CH227210).
- The named person must normally be named in the notice and given a **copy at the same time** the notice is issued (Sch 36 para 4; CH23640), unless the tribunal approves the notice and disapplies the copy requirement because copying would prejudice the assessment or collection of tax (Sch 36 para 4; CH23660). "Prejudice" means grounds to believe a risk to effective assessment or collection (collusion, intimidation, flight, alienation of assets, destruction of evidence); "seriously prejudice" means a major negative impact, e.g. tipping off a person suspected of a serious civil offence (CH24220; CH24240).
- **Deceased persons:** a third party notice to check the tax position of a dead person cannot be given more than 4 years after the death (CH23620).
- **Banks:** HMRC officers must never make an informal request to a bank (CH23100; CH227210). The sequence is: ask the taxpayer (formally, with a taxpayer notice if necessary, and consider a penalty for no response); then a signed bank mandate for a direct approach, sent to the bank's central/customer service address, with no copy to the person (CH227110; CH227130); then a FIN or a tribunal-approved third party notice. A mandate only obtains material the bank would have given the person, not the bank's internal confidential records (CH227110 caveat). Tribunal approval is required for a notice to a bank except where HMRC's commitment to UK Finance applies or a FIN is used (CH227210; CH227260). An **opportunity letter** precedes a tribunal-approved notice to a bank, and a **Summary of Reasons** letter (SEES IIP29) goes to the person whose position is being checked (CH227210; CH227230).
- **Pensions matters** (registered pension scheme, annuity purchased with scheme sums, EFRBS, QROPS/former QROPS): no tribunal or taxpayer approval is needed and no copy need go to the taxpayer, but a copy generally goes to the scheme administrator, responsible person or scheme manager unless the notice is given to a "prescribed description of person"; appeal rights are restricted (Sch 36 paras 34B, 34C; CH23840). There is **no** right of appeal against a tribunal-approved pensions-matter notice even though approval is not normally required.
- **Partnerships:** where a nominated partner does not meet an informal request for the partnership books and records during an enquiry, HMRC uses a formal Sch 36 notice (EM7566; CH23720).

### 2.6 Approvals and internal authorisation

An **authorised officer** is an HMRC officer with authority to undertake certain actions and to agree the use of certain powers by others; only a small number hold the status, and different officers are authorised for different purposes (CH261000). Minimum grade for most actions is SO (CH262100). The requesting officer must make a written report (template IIP35) covering the action needed, the facts, justification, alternatives considered, and HRA/proportionality analysis (CH260500); the authorised officer must record risks, factors and reasons, and sign and date the submission (CH261200). Urgent verbal authorisation is possible but must be written up on return to the office (CH262100).

CH260100 distinguishes **statutory** approval requirements from **HMRC-imposed policy** requirements:

| Action | Approval requirement | Statutory or policy |
|---|---|---|
| Application to the tribunal to approve a notice or inspection | Authorised officer agreement | Statutory (Sch 36 para 59; CH21720) |
| Third party notice requiring tribunal approval | Authorised officer agreement | Statutory (CH24160) |
| Notice for documents more than 6 years old | Authorised officer agreement | Statutory (Sch 36 para 20; CH22140) |
| Issuing a FIN | Designated authorised officer | Statutory (CH23103; CH23120) |
| Inspection without at least 7 days' notice and without consent | Authorised officer agreement | Statutory (CH262400) |
| Inspection at a person's home, or where private use of the premises is in doubt | Authorised officer agreement | **HMRC policy** (CH254540) |
| Covert activity | Authorised officer agreement | **HMRC policy** (CH207500) |
| Penalty for deliberately obstructing a tribunal-approved inspection | Authorised officer agreement | **HMRC policy** (CH270100) |
| Tax-related penalty via application to the Upper Tribunal | Authorised officer agreement | (CH270400) |

Note CH260300's clarification: issuing a taxpayer notice for a document **not** more than 6 years old does not need authorised officer approval.

### 2.7 Tribunal approval of a taxpayer notice

Tribunal approval is **not usually required** for a taxpayer notice. HMRC seeks it discretionarily where, for example, time limits are at risk, the person is uncooperative, the status of documents as statutory records is disputed, third-party information needs protecting, or there is a risk of document destruction or fraud (CH223400). Two consequences follow:

1. Obtaining tribunal approval **removes the person's right of appeal** against the notice (CH223400; CH24320).
2. HMRC guidance is that the person should usually be told the information is required and given a reasonable opportunity to make representations before approval is sought (CH223400).

### 2.9 Penalties under Schedule 36

| Trigger | Penalty | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Failure to provide information or produce documents by the end of the period stated in the notice, without reasonable excuse | £300 initial | Sch 36 para 39; CH26220; CH26620; CH26640 |
| Continuing failure after an initial penalty has been assessed | up to £60 per day | Sch 36 para 40; CH26620; CH26660 |
| Continuing failure to comply with an **identity unknown** notice, after daily penalties have already been charged | up to £1,000 per day | CH26620; CH26705 |
| Deliberate obstruction of a **tribunal-approved** inspection | £300, plus up to £60 per day if obstruction continues | CH25320; CH255520; CH26240 |

Sequencing constraint (important for implementation): daily, increased daily and tax-related penalties **cannot** be charged unless an initial penalty has first been assessed, and an increased daily penalty requires daily penalties already to have been charged (note on CH26620). The £3,000 inaccuracy maximum is described by HMRC as reserved for very serious cases of deliberate inaccuracy, not a standard amount.

Document retention after production (Sch 36 para 54; CH26260; CH26265): a document may be destroyed unless HMRC has advised in writing it must be kept, or unless fewer than 6 months have passed since the person was last told the document may be included in a notice and it has not since been included.

Deliberate obstruction is defined by HMRC as being aware an officer is conducting a tribunal-approved inspection and preventing them from doing anything reasonably and legitimately needed to complete it (CH26240). The "course of an inspection" begins at the agreed or notice-specified date and time and ends when the officer indicates completion or the tribunal-allowed time expires, whichever is earlier (CH26240).

Where a Self Assessment return is more than 12 months late, HMRC may use Sch 36 powers to gather information to establish liability and to consider whether information was deliberately withheld (CH205335; CH401223; CH402254) — that being the route to the higher tax-geared late-filing penalties under Sch 55 FA 2009.

## 3. Inspection powers

### 3.1 What can be inspected

Schedule 36 para 10 allows an officer to enter a person's **business premises** and inspect the premises, business assets and business documents (CH25180 and following):

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Premises | Any building or structure, any land, and any means of transport — not necessarily standing on land owned by the same person (CH25180) |
| Business premises | Premises there is reason to believe are used in connection with the carrying on of a business by, or on behalf of, a person. If only part is so used, only that part is business premises (CH25180) |
| Carrying on a business | Normal everyday meaning; includes any trade, profession or undertaking alone or in partnership, some charity trading activities, the letting of property, public authorities, and entities required to be or registered for VAT (Sch 36 para 60; CH25200). No regulations further defining this exist (CH25200) |
| Business assets | Physical items believed owned, leased or used in connection with carrying on a business by any person; **excludes documents** unless they are trading stock or plant (Sch 36 para 10(3)&(4); CH25260) |
| Business documents | Documents or copies that relate to the carrying on of a business by any person **and** are part of that person's statutory records — both conditions must be met (Sch 36 para 10(3); CH25280) |

Limits (CH255510 group; CH25140):

- Inspection reaches only statutory records **physically on the premises at the time**. Non-statutory information can be examined only with consent or via an information notice.
- Restricted documents (section 2.4 above) cannot be inspected even if offered, unless offered despite a reminder (Sch 36 para 14; CH25300).
- Officers must not use force or subterfuge to gain entry, and must not search, rummage or wander unaccompanied without consent (CH255520; CH255525).
- No power to compel a business to cash up at any time, and no power to inspect cash unless the cash is **trading stock** (e.g. currency exchangers, coin dealers) (CH258400; CH259150).
- Questions during an inspection are limited to matters relating to the items inspected, and the person **is not obliged to answer**. A penalty risk for refusing arises only where the inspection is tribunal-approved (CH25320).

### 3.2 Dwellings

There is no power to enter or inspect any part of premises used **solely** as a private dwelling, except for valuation purposes (Sch 36 para 10(2); CH25240; CH25380). A private residence can nevertheless be business premises where the business is run from the home, business assets are stored there, business records are kept there, or the home is registered as the principal place of business for VAT (CH254510).

HMRC states there is **no** power to inspect where a director or employee merely works from home, where records have been temporarily taken home, or where rooms are rented that are used solely as someone else's home (CH254510). Even where a partner works from home such that it becomes partnership business premises, HMRC's guidance is that a visit is justified only in exceptional circumstances.

For a **records-only** home visit, HMRC's internal requirements are: line manager agreement; a clear statement that the person can refuse; and issue of factsheet CC/FS1 (a, b, c or d) — explicitly **not** CC/FS3, which is for visits under inspection powers (CH254510).

### 3.3 Notice and approval

At least **7 days' prior notice** is required for a Sch 36 visit unless authorised officer agreement is obtained, or the visit is at the customer's request or with their consent (CH262400; CH260100). Tribunal approval should be sought before invigilation if the customer is expected to object (CH258400; CH25540). If a customer asks an officer to leave during an invigilation exercise, the officer must leave (CH258400).

### 3.4 Removed records

HMRC's procedures on removal (CH255545–CH255555): give a written receipt, describing records carefully (e.g. using "said to relate to"); allow the person to take a copy, and provide a free copy without delay if reasonably required; store removed records securely and not retain them longer than necessary; return them at the earliest opportunity, by recorded or registered delivery if posted, or against a receipt if in person. Where records cannot be returned (person missing, insolvent), destruction requires written line manager authority and only after the statutory preservation period.

Removal of computers and media should only happen in exceptional circumstances, and requires the officer to record why removal rather than on-premises copying is the only way to secure the records (CH214100). Only an "authorised person" under FA 2008 s.114 — an HMRC officer with appropriate training, or a specialist consultant — may access computer equipment to check its operation; other officers must seek their assistance and must not attempt it themselves (CH218400; EM2809). HMRC officers are not entitled to demand direct access to a taxpayer's computer system; they must ask the person authorised by management to operate it (EM2805 caveats). CCTV footage is treated as a document under Part 7 Chapter 1 FA 2008 (CH214200; CH23320); officers may view only pre-recorded footage, never live footage.

## 4. Adjacent powers

**Bulk data-gathering — Sch 23 FA 2011.** HMRC can require a "relevant data-holder" (20 statutory types, including a person who ceased to be one less than four years before the notice) to provide specified relevant data (CH28410; SI 2012/847). Categories relevant to SA-type income include land lessees, occupiers, users and managing agents (para 18), securities registrars and payers (paras 14, 15, 19), public funds grant/subsidy payers (para 16), licence issuers and register maintainers (para 17), auctioneers and dealers in tangible moveable property where disposal value exceeds **£6,000** (para 20; SI 2012/847 reg 18), Lloyd's managing agents (para 21), and plan managers and child trust fund account providers (para 22). Appeal is within 30 days of the notice, in writing, stating grounds; there is no appeal against a requirement for the data-holder's own statutory records or against a tribunal-approved notice, and the tribunal's decision is final despite ss.11 and 13 TCEA 2007 (para 29; CH29430; CH29440). Penalties mirror Sch 36: £300 initial (para 30), up to £60/day (para 31), up to £1,000/day with tribunal approval (paras 38–39), and up to £3,000 for careless or deliberate inaccuracy (CH29510–CH29550). A "material document" must be preserved; it ceases to be material once the notice is complied with (unless HMRC says to preserve it) or after 6 months from being told a notice is likely (para 30; CH29530).

**Dishonest tax agents — Sch 38 FA 2012.** A **file access notice** can require a tax agent, or a third-party document-holder, to provide "relevant documents" — the agent's working papers and other documents received, created, prepared or used to assist clients with tax affairs (para 7; CH182540; CH182600). Available in **Case A** (a conduct notice has been issued and appeal rights have expired or been exhausted) and **Case B** (conviction for a tax-related offence involving fraud or dishonesty committed after becoming a tax agent, with appeal rights against the conviction expired or exhausted within the last 12 months) (CH182540). Tribunal approval is required in both cases (para 8) and the tribunal's approval decision is final, although a third-party document-holder (not the agent) may appeal on the ground that the notice is unduly onerous. Pre-1 April 2013 documents can be requested but cannot be used to determine the amount of a dishonest conduct penalty — only to correct client tax records and inform investigation decisions (CH182540). HMRC's practice is to make an informal approach before issuing a formal notice (CH882300).

## 5. Safeguards

**Human Rights Act.** HMRC's guidance, relying on *Ferrazzini v Italy* (ECHR 44759/98), is that Article 6 does **not** apply to the determination of tax liability or to related procedural steps such as information and inspection notices, and cannot be relied on to refuse compliance on right-to-silence grounds (CH21320). Article 8 intrusion is lawful only if in accordance with law, necessary in a democratic society and in pursuit of a legitimate aim, and must be reasonable and proportionate (CH21340; *Amann v Switzerland* 27798/95).

Article 6 **does** engage for behaviour-based penalties, which HMRC treats procedurally as "criminal" (CH300200; CH300500; EM1350). Before asking about a person's behaviour, an officer must give the HRA message — via factsheet CC/FS9 or verbally — together with the appropriate penalties factsheet (CH402350; EM1350). CH402350 goes further and states that although the law permits use of Sch 36 powers to obtain evidence of penalty behaviour, it is unlikely they can be used in practice because of the privilege against self-incrimination; information powers can only obtain documents and information that already **exist**, not compel answers about *why* or *how* an inaccuracy occurred. No such restriction applies to third party notices. Refusing to cooperate is not itself evidence of deliberate behaviour, though it may support a reasonable inference alongside other evidence.

**Procedural safeguards (HMRC's own instructions, not statute).**

- Officers must complete required training to the required standard and obtain necessary authorisation before using these powers (CH201300).
- Officers must ensure information and documents requested are reasonably required **solely** for checking the person's tax position (CH201300; CH21620).
- An information notice should normally be used only after an informal request has failed, unless specific exceptions apply (CH223100; EM1580). The standard informal period is normally 30 days as a minimum, with an extra week for requests to agents where the enquiry is opened 1–20 December and an extra two weeks for 21 December–31 January (EM1580).
- Factsheet **CC/FS2** "Information notices" must accompany an information notice — and only an information notice under Sch 36; CC/FS16 is used for excise powers instead (CH221000; CH223500; note 40).
- Factsheet CC/FS1 (a, b, c or d) accompanies the opening letter of a compliance check into a Self Assessment return; CC/FS3 accompanies a visit by agreement or with advance notice; CC/FS4 an unannounced inspection; CC/FS5 a tribunal-approved unannounced inspection; CC/FS22 a request for electronic records (CH207330; CH253000; note 40).
- Officers must not visit a person's home unless requested, or unless part of it is business premises with a reasonable requirement to inspect (CH207400).
- Officers must avoid prematurely issuing protective assessments, determinations or closure notices in Self Assessment checks to force progress before fact-finding is complete (CH207340).
- Covert surveillance likely to obtain private information requires a Directed Surveillance Authority under RIPA, must be shown to be necessary and proportionate with no less intrusive alternative, and **must not** be carried out at anyone's home regardless of other conditions (CH207510; CH214200).
- Meetings with a person under 18 require a "suitable person" aged 18+ present who has agreed to oversee the young person's wellbeing, with their name, capacity and consent recorded (CH201350).
- Confidentiality: disclosure in breach of s.18(1) CRCA 2005 risks criminal prosecution under s.19 CRCA (CH871000).
- Since a change taking effect 12 February 2019, HMRC treats **voluntary returns** (received without HMRC having issued a notice to file) as made in response to a notice to file given on the date the return was received, retrospectively and prospectively (CH820000; SAM121140) — which affects when enquiry windows and record-retention deadlines run.

## 6. Machine-implementable rule statements

Each rule below is stated as inputs → condition → result, drawn from the sources cited.

**R1 — Record retention deadline (business).** Inputs: `carries_on_trade` (incl. property letting), `return_is_for_tax_year`, `year_of_assessment`, `period_end`, `filing_date`, `filed_on_time`, `enquiry_opened`, `enquiry_completed_date`.
Result: retain until `max(baseline, enquiry_close)` where `baseline = 5th anniversary of 31 Jan following year_of_assessment` if `return_is_for_tax_year` else `6th anniversary of period_end`; and `enquiry_close = enquiry_completed_date` if `enquiry_opened` else `filing_date + 12 months` if `filed_on_time` else `first anniversary of next quarter day after filing_date`. (TMA 1970 s.12B; CH14530)

**R2 — Record retention deadline (non-business).** As R1 but `baseline = 1st anniversary of 31 Jan following year_of_assessment`. (CH14550)

**R3 — Record-keeping penalty ceiling.** Inputs: `count_of_returns_or_claims_with_failure`. Result: maximum penalty = `£3,000 × count`, one failure per return or claim; may be reduced below maximum. (TMA 1970 s.12B(5), s.100(1); EM4650)

**R4 — Taxpayer notice permissibility for a period covered by an SA return.** Inputs: `sa_return_made_for_period`, `enquiry_notice_given` (s.9A/s.12AC/Sch1A para 5), `closure_notice_given_for_matter` (s.28A/s.28C/Sch1A para 57), `reason_to_suspect_under_assessment`, `correctable_within_time_limits`, `checking_other_tax_or_repayment`.
Result: notice permitted if `NOT sa_return_made_for_period` OR `(enquiry_notice_given AND NOT closure_notice_given_for_matter)` OR `(reason_to_suspect_under_assessment AND correctable_within_time_limits)` OR `checking_other_tax_or_repayment`. (Sch 36 para 21; CH23540)

**R5 — Authorised officer agreement for old documents.** Input: `earliest_document_origination_date`, `notice_date`, `is_qrops_notice`. Condition: `notice_date − earliest_document_origination_date > 6 years` (or `> 10 years` if `is_qrops_notice`). Result: authorised officer agreement required; absent it, non-production of that document is **not** a failure to comply. (Sch 36 paras 20; CH22140; CH23990)

**R6 — Appeal right against an information notice requirement.** Inputs: `tribunal_approved`, `requirement_is_statutory_records`, `notice_type`. Result: no appeal if `tribunal_approved` OR `requirement_is_statutory_records`; third party notices otherwise appealable only on "unduly onerous" grounds; deadline 30 days from the date on the notice. (Sch 36 paras 29–32; CH24320; CH24340)

**R7 — Sch 36 penalty ladder.** Inputs: `initial_penalty_assessed`, `daily_penalties_charged`, `notice_type`, `days_of_continuing_failure`. Result: `£300` on failure/obstruction; daily up to `£60 × days` only if `initial_penalty_assessed`; up to `£1,000/day` only if `notice_type == identity_unknown AND daily_penalties_charged`. No penalty imposed during the appeal period or while an appeal against the notice is open. (Sch 36 paras 39, 40; CH26620; CH26705; CH223800)

**R8 — Inspection scope test for a home.** Inputs: `business_run_from_home`, `business_assets_stored`, `business_records_kept`, `vat_principal_place_of_business`, `merely_employee_working_from_home`, `records_temporarily_at_home`, `room_solely_someone_elses_dwelling`.
Result: premises are inspectable business premises only if any of the first four is true and none of the last three applies; parts used **solely** as a dwelling are never inspectable (except for valuation). (Sch 36 para 10(2); CH25240; CH254510)

**R9 — Inspection notice period.** Inputs: `days_notice_given`, `authorised_officer_agreed`, `at_customer_request_or_consent`. Result: permitted if `days_notice_given >= 7` OR `authorised_officer_agreed` OR `at_customer_request_or_consent`. (CH262400)

## 7. Gaps in the sources

- **Making Tax Digital for Income Tax record-keeping and digital record requirements.** MTD appears only incidentally in helpsheets (HS342, HS340, HS236) to say that claims are made through compatible software instead of SA boxes, and that qualifying care receipt customers are exempt for 2026-27. No MTD digital record-keeping obligations are in the notes.
- **Time limits for HMRC to assess a s.12B(5) record-keeping penalty**, and the procedure for its determination and appeal beyond the bare statement that normal appeal rights apply.
- **Whether records must be kept in original form or copies suffice** for income tax and CGT (the notes cover this only for VAT and computer records).
- **The specific Schedule 36 paragraph numbers** for some penalties: the notes give paragraph references for the initial (para 39), daily (para 40) and inaccuracy (para 40A) penalties, but the increased daily penalty for identity unknown notices is cited in the notes only by manual page (CH26705), with para 49A appearing in a cross-reference list without confirmation.

- **Reasonable excuse for Sch 36 failures** is repeatedly referenced (CH26320, CH26220) but its content is not set out in the notes.
- **Partnership record-keeping obligations** specifically: the notes confirm partnerships are within the definition of "person" for direct tax record purposes (CH10400) and that a nominated partner can be compelled by a Sch 36 notice (EM7566), but not what records a partnership must keep or for how long.

- Several source sections are expressly **withheld under FOIA 2000 exemptions** (notably CH206250 on handling third-party information, CH22200 on personal records head office contact, CH261200 on authorised officer sign-off, CH223150/CH223300, EM4655, parts of CH207200/CH207400 and CH214200), so the guidance reproduced here is incomplete on those points by construction.
