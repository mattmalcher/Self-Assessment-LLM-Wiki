This page covers the dispute route for Self Assessment income tax: which HMRC decisions carry a right of appeal, how and when an appeal is made, late appeals, HMRC's statutory review, postponement of disputed tax, ADR, and the procedure that governs an appeal once it reaches the First-tier Tribunal (Tax Chamber). It distinguishes statute (mainly TMA 1970 and SI 2009/273) from HMRC's own manual guidance (ARTG, SAM, EM, CH), which is HMRC's interpretation and internal practice rather than law. This is an independent reference page, not an HMRC publication.

## 1. The basic shape of a direct tax dispute

For direct taxes the sequence is: **appeal to HMRC first**, then either (a) settle by agreement, (b) take an internal review, or (c) notify the appeal to the First-tier Tribunal (FTT).

ARTG2410 states that a customer must send a written appeal to HMRC before notifying a direct tax appeal to the tribunal. If an appellant goes straight to the Tribunals Service without having appealed to HMRC, ARTG2440 says the tribunal lacks jurisdiction and HMRC's litigator applies to strike the appeal out (unless a late appeal to HMRC is accepted).

A review and a tribunal appeal are mutually exclusive at any one time: ARTG4640 says the customer cannot appeal to the tribunal, and the tribunal cannot consider an appeal, while a review is ongoing.

## 2. What can be appealed

An **appealable decision** is a formal notice — assessment, amendment, closure notice, determination, information notice or direction — where a right of appeal is given specifically by statute (ARTG2190). ARTG2160 lists the appealable decisions but states expressly that the list is **not exhaustive**.

Decisions relevant to Self Assessment that carry an appeal right include:

| Decision | Appeal provision / note |
|---|---|
| HMRC amendment of a self assessment (closure notice conclusions/amendments) | TMA 1970 s.31(1)(b), s.31A; appeal within 30 days of issue of the closure notice (SALF; ARTG) |
| Discovery assessment (s.29 TMA 1970) | ARTG2160; SAM20080 (appeal recorded, stand-over considered) |
| Jeopardy amendment (s.9C TMA 1970) | Appealable to HMRC, but see restriction below |
| Penalties and (for 2009-10 and earlier) surcharges | ARTG2215; TMA 1970 s.59C(7)-(10) for surcharges |
| Refusal of a claim for relief against double assessment (s.32) | Appeal within 30 days after notice of refusal (TMA 1970 s.32(3)(b)) |

### What cannot be appealed

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| A Simple Assessment — unless the taxpayer first raises a query under s.31AA TMA 1970 (within 60 days of issue, or a longer period HMRC allows) and receives a final response | TMA 1970 s.31AA(2)-(3), (10); ARTG2040; ARTG2212 |
| A determination of tax (no appeal right); the remedy is to file the outstanding return, which displaces it | EM1982 |
| Notices under TMA 1970 s.9A, s.12AC, FA 1998 Sch 18 para 24, FA 2007 Sch 24 para 5(1) — no appeal right, but the taxpayer may apply to the tribunal for closure at any time after issue | EM1976 |
| HMRC's decision on a postponement application itself (no appeal or review; only referral to the tribunal on the amount) | ARTG2510 |
| The tribunal's decision on a postponement application — final and conclusive notwithstanding TCEA 2007 ss.11 and 13 | TMA 1970 s.55; EM1951 also notes no statutory review of a tribunal postponement decision |
| Certain tribunal decisions on applications (Sch 36 FA 2008 applications, hardship, postponement, late appeals, closure applications) | ARTG2040 |
| Ex parte (without notice) HMRC applications — usually not appealable | ARTG2040 |
| Interest itself — ARTG/CH state there is no statutory right of appeal against interest; only the underlying tax is appealable, and the interest adjusts automatically | CH140260 (CH140300 distinguishes an "enquiry" about interest from an "objection") |
| A partner's own self assessment as consequentially amended following a partnership Revenue amendment | SAM (Individual or trust) — "no right of appeal against the consequential amendment" |
| Approval of a data-holder notice by the tribunal — final, notwithstanding TCEA 2007 ss.11 and 13 | CH28270 |

**Scope limit on closure notice appeals.** ARTG2190 and ARTG4070 both state that an appeal against a closure notice is limited to the specific conclusions and amendments given effect by that notice, and that the review process cannot expand that scope.

**Jeopardy amendments.** A taxpayer may appeal to HMRC within 30 days against a s.9C jeopardy amendment (EM1955), but ARTG2150/EM1955 say the appeal cannot be notified to the tribunal, nor a review requested, until the enquiry is closed by a partial or final closure notice. Only postponement decisions can be taken to the tribunal during the enquiry (EM1951).

**Judicial review** is the route where there is no statutory appeal right — e.g. discretionary decisions, application of Extra-Statutory Concessions, misdirection, or unfair conduct by an officer (ARTG12010). ARTG10020 notes the Upper Tribunal is not a court for Human Rights Act s.4 purposes and cannot make a declaration of incompatibility.

## 3. Who can appeal

ARTG2150: the person named in the decision, a personal representative or trustee, a partner, or an authorised agent. Notes:

- A **bankrupt individual** loses standing to appeal tax debts provable in the bankruptcy; only the trustee in bankruptcy may act (ARTG2150).
- A **struck-off company** cannot appeal, as it is no longer a legal person; an insolvent or in-administration company appeals via its insolvency practitioner or administrator (ARTG3030).
- A **partnership** appeal (e.g. against a partnership late-filing penalty) must be made by the nominated partner or authorised agent; SAM10130 says HMRC officers should refuse an appeal not so made and ask the nominated partner to make it.

## 4. Making the appeal: form, grounds and time limit

**Statute.** A notice of appeal under s.31 must be given **in writing to the relevant officer**, must **specify the grounds of appeal**, and must be given **within 30 days after the "specified date"** (TMA 1970 s.31A(1), (5)).

The "specified date" depends on what is being appealed (TMA 1970 s.31A(2)-(4A)):

| Appeal type | Specified date |
|---|---|
| Amendment (s.31(1)(a)/(c)) | Date the notice of amendment was issued |
| Closure notice conclusions/amendments (s.31(1)(b)) | Date the closure notice was issued |
| Assessment other than a simple assessment (s.31(1)(d)) | Date the notice of assessment was issued |
| Simple assessment (s.31(1)(d)) | Date the person was given the final response to a s.31AA query |

**HMRC's gloss.** ARTG2180 says the 30 days runs from the date HMRC **posts** the decision notice, not the date the customer receives it. ARTG2430/2440, less precisely, describe the 30 days as running from the date the customer *receives* the formal decision notice. The statute (s.31A) fixes the period by reference to the date of issue of the relevant notice, which supports the ARTG2180 formulation.

**Late-filing penalty appeals.** SAM10080 records an operational window of **37 days** (30 statutory days plus 7 days for printing and despatch of the notice) — an HMRC administrative allowance, not a change to the statutory 30 days.

**Grounds.** ARTG2171 requires the decision maker to contact the customer where grounds appear invalid and seek valid grounds, or withdrawal/amendment of the appeal. If invalid grounds are maintained, ARTG2172 says HMRC applies to the tribunal for strike-out and the tribunal decides validity. ARTG2150's Example 2 indicates that grounds expressed only as general "unfairness" are not automatically valid; specificity is required.

**Machine-implementable form:**

```
Input: decision_type, date_of_issue, date_appeal_received, grounds_present (bool)
appeal_in_time := date_appeal_received <= date_of_issue + 30 days
appeal_valid   := written AND grounds_present AND appeal_in_time
                  (else late-appeal test at §5)
```

**Non-response.** ARTG2220 says that if the customer does not reply to a formal decision notice within 30 days, the decision stands.

## 5. Late appeals

Two distinct tests apply, depending on who is deciding.

**HMRC (statutory test).** Under TMA 1970 s.49(3), a decision maker **must** accept a late appeal where the customer shows a reasonable excuse for the lateness and appealed without unreasonable delay after the excuse ceased (ARTG2215; ARTG2180).

ARTG2215 gives HMRC's view on reasonable excuse for late appeals, with two firm points:
- The **amount of tax involved is explicitly not relevant** to whether an excuse is reasonable.
- The examples list is **a guide, not exhaustive**.

Explicitly **not** reasonable excuses in HMRC's view (ARTG2215): the agent's failure to act in time; complexity of the taxpayer's affairs; being too busy; and non-liability or overpayment of tax.

**The tribunal (wider test).** ARTG2215 states that the tribunal, unlike an HMRC decision maker, is **not limited by the reasonable excuse conditions** and can admit a late appeal if it is in the interests of justice.

**Procedure.** If HMRC does not accept a late appeal, the customer applies to the tribunal for permission to give a late notice of appeal (ARTG8240; ARTG8240 notes the application is included on the appeal form). Where the notice of appeal to the tribunal is itself late, rule 20(4) SI 2009/273 requires the notice to include a request for permission and the reason for the delay. TMA 1970 s.49G(3) likewise requires the customer notifying an appeal to the tribunal outside the statutory time limits to ask the tribunal's permission and explain the delay.

ARTG8240 says HMRC decides whether to **oppose** a late appeal application by reference to the length of delay, whether there is a reasonable excuse, and the likely tribunal view.

## 6. HMRC's internal review

The review is a statutory process conducted by a **review officer** who is not the decision maker (ARTG000). ARTG000 says all decisions other than restoration decisions are subject to a right of internal review, whether or not HMRC offers one. In direct tax HMRC **may** offer a review where it is not possible to finalise an appeal by agreement; in indirect tax a review **must** be offered with an appealable decision (ARTG000) — the "must offer" rule is therefore not the SA position.

### Triggers and the "view of the matter"

Before a review can begin or be validly offered, HMRC must send the customer its **current view of the matter in question** (TMA 1970 s.49B(2) for a customer-requested review; s.49C(2) for an HMRC-offered review). ARTG2215 says that if HMRC's offer of a review does not include a valid view of the matter, **the offer is invalid** and a fresh offer must be made; the customer retains the right to notify the tribunal instead of accepting the fresh offer.

Where the customer requests a review after appealing, the decision maker must write setting out HMRC's most recent view of the matter within **30 days** of the review request (or other reasonable time) (s.49B(2) TMA 1970; ARTG2213), and internally must submit the case to the Legal Group review team within **7 calendar days** of notifying that view (ARTG2213 — internal timing, not a statutory deadline).

### Review timetable

| Step | Period | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Customer accepts review offer or notifies tribunal | 30 days from the date of the review offer letter | s.49C TMA 1970; ARTG2215; ARTG4220 |
| Review period (direct tax, review offer accepted) | 45 days from the date HMRC receives acceptance | ARTG4690 |
| Review period (direct tax, customer-requested review) | 45 days from the date of the decision maker's letter giving HMRC's latest view | ARTG4690 |
| Review period where acceptance was late | 45 days from the date HMRC decides to undertake the review | ARTG4690 (see ARTG4300) |
| Extension | By agreement with the customer if the review officer cannot complete in time | ARTG4690 |
| Customer notifies tribunal after conclusion of review | 30 days from the date of the conclusion of review letter | s.49F/s.49G(2), (5)(a) TMA 1970; ARTG4820 |
| Where HMRC misses the review period | Customer may notify the tribunal from the day after expiry of the review period until 30 days from the date of HMRC's letter stating the conclusion the review is treated as having reached | ARTG4850 |

Note the two different 45-day start dates HMRC gives: ARTG2216 says the 45-day review period begins **from the date the "view of the matter" letter was sent to the customer**, while ARTG4690 states it begins from the date HMRC receives the customer's acceptance (offered review) or the date of the decision maker's latest-view letter (customer-requested review). ARTG4690 is the more detailed statement.

### Outcomes and consequences

Review outcomes (ARTG4810): **upheld** (decision stands unaltered), **varied** (changed in some way — e.g. assessment amended, reasons changed), or **cancelled** (decision found inappropriate — e.g. assessment withdrawn). The conclusion of review letter must state the decision, the reasons, the customer's next steps and, where upheld or varied, the appeal options, time limits and consequences (ARTG4820; ARTG4690).

**Deemed settlement.** If the customer neither accepts a review offer nor notifies the tribunal within 30 days, the appeal is **treated as settled by agreement** on HMRC's stated view (TMA 1970 s.49C(4); ARTG4070; ARTG2730). The same consequence follows if the customer does not notify the tribunal within 30 days of the conclusion of review letter (s.49F TMA 1970; ARTG2730). ARTG3420 notes that a review conclusion letter treated as a s.49F/s.54(1) agreement does **not** carry the customer's 30-day withdrawal right (s.49F(3) TMA 1970).

**Non-reviewable decisions** (ARTG4050): decisions with no right of appeal; decisions on a late review or late appeal; refusals of postponement or hardship applications; conclusions of review; and simple assessments unless HMRC has given a final response to a s.31AA query.

**Scope and standard.** ARTG4080 says the review officer checks whether the decision is in line with legal and technical guidance, policy and practice, applying HMRC's Litigation and Settlement Strategy factors (evidence, technical/legal merits, materiality, defendability, wider implications). ARTG4070 notes HMRC may in exceptional circumstances uphold a decision even where the chance of litigation success is under 50%, provided it is defendable and evidence-based, and that the review officer has no discretion to depart from current policy or practice without prior approval from policy and technical teams. Where the decision followed a Case Board or Issues Panel, ARTG4635 requires the review officer to clear a proposed variation or cancellation with the secretariat before issuing the conclusion.

## 7. Settling an appeal without a tribunal decision

**Section 54 agreement.** An appeal may be settled by agreement between HMRC and the customer at any time before the tribunal decides, that the decision be upheld, varied in a particular manner, or cancelled (s.54(1) TMA 1970; ARTG2720). It requires mutual offer and acceptance — ARTG2720 says a notice of appeal on its own is not usually an offer to settle, and that the offer/acceptance process must be followed even where HMRC accepts the taxpayer's grounds. Verbal agreements must be confirmed in writing (s.54(3) TMA 1970). The customer has **30 days** from the agreement (or from written confirmation of a verbal agreement) to notify HMRC in writing that they no longer agree (s.54(2) TMA 1970); ARTG2750 says there is no provision to reopen a s.54 agreement outside that window.

**Withdrawal.** The customer may withdraw an appeal; a decision maker who considers withdrawal inappropriate must write to the customer within **30 days** of the withdrawal (ARTG2740; ARTG3430, which adds that a copy goes to the Tribunals Service).

**Routes to finality.** EM6002 lists four routes to a final settled position: binding contract settlement acceptance; the taxpayer not appealing HMRC's decision notices; exhaustion of tribunal/court appeal rights; or a s.54 TMA 1970 agreement.

**Contract settlements** (SAM31060) are letters of offer from the taxpayer accepted in writing by HMRC, sweeping additional liability, interest, surcharge and penalties for all affected years into one settlement, possibly by instalments. EM6002 states VAT and VAT penalties must never be included in a contract settlement. CH140310 notes that once a contract settlement is agreed, the statutory FA 2009 interest provisions no longer apply and contractual interest terms apply instead.

## 8. Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)

ADR is a collaborative process using an independent HMRC facilitator to help the parties explore resolution or prepare for litigation. CH280400 states expressly that ADR **does not affect existing review and appeal rights**.

| Aspect | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Restricted to "SME, individuals and CITEX" sectors; used where parties have reached an impasse | CH280400 |

## 9. Postponement of tax pending appeal

### The statutory rule

Absent postponement, tax charged by the amendment or assessment under appeal is **due and payable as if there were no appeal** (TMA 1970 s.55). The appellant who believes they are overcharged may apply in writing for a determination of the amount to be postponed.

| Step | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Application for postponement | In writing, within **30 days** of the "specified date", stating the amount believed overcharged and the grounds | s.55(3)(a) TMA 1970; ARTG2510 |
| "Specified date" | Date of issue of the notice of amendment or assessment; for an appeal against a closure notice conclusion/amendment, date of issue of the closure notice | s.55(10A) TMA 1970 |
| Late application on changed circumstances | Permitted under s.55(3A) | s.55(3A) TMA 1970 |
| Referral to tribunal if HMRC's amount not agreed | Within **30 days** from the date of the document notifying HMRC's decision | s.55(3)(b) TMA 1970; ARTG2530; SAM (para 11) |
| Revised determination | Either party may apply to the tribunal at any time before determination of the appeal, on a change of circumstances, where they cannot agree | s.55(4) TMA 1970 |
| Finality | Tribunal's postponement decision is final and conclusive notwithstanding TCEA 2007 ss.11 and 13 | s.55 TMA 1970 |

### Conditions and limits (HMRC's operational reading)

- **A valid appeal is a precondition.** ARTG2510 states a postponement application cannot be accepted without a related appeal; if one arrives alone, the decision maker checks the SA record or contacts the customer.
- **No formal right for penalties, surcharges or interest.** SAM11020 says there is no legal right to formally postpone charges arising from penalties, surcharge or interest; HMRC's policy (ARTG2510; ARTG2570; SAM11090) is to **informally stand over** the full amount pending settlement of the appeal.
- **No formal right for payments on account.** SAM (Informal standover) says there is no legal right to apply for payments on account to be formally stood over, though a separate claim to reduce them can be made.
- **Accelerated payment notices override postponement.** Tax subject to an APN cannot be postponed and any existing postponement ceases to have effect (ARTG2510; TMA 1970 s.55(8B)-(8E)). Payment is due on or before the last day of 90 days beginning with the day the notice is given, or, where representations were made, the later of that day and 30 days from notification of HMRC's determination on the representations (s.55(8D)).
- **Simple assessment.** Postponement of a simple assessment ends once a final response is given under s.31AA(9); a fresh s.55(3) application is needed if the customer then appeals (ARTG2510).
- **Formal vs informal standover.** A **formal standover** is a legally based postponement, requires a written application from a person entitled to appeal, applies only to charges from a Revenue assessment, Revenue amendment or jeopardy amendment, defers due dates (affecting surcharge computation) but **not** the reckonable date for interest, and is automatically cancelled if the underlying charge is amended (SAM11010; SAM11040). An **informal standover** is discretionary, needs no application, does not affect the interest reckonable date or the surcharge due date but suspends surcharge calculation while recorded, and cannot replace a formal standover where one is available (SAM11010; SAM11070).

### Interest still runs

ARTG2560 states interest is payable on tax and Class 4 NIC paid late, **including amounts that have been postponed**. CH143280 (with TMA 1970 s.55 and FA 2009 Sch 53 para 4) puts it as: late payment interest on any postponed amount that ultimately becomes payable runs from the date that would have applied absent the appeal — for a balancing payment, the original balancing payment due date.

**Machine-implementable:** postponement suspends *collection*, not *interest accrual*. Interest start date = the statutory relevant date for the charge, ignoring the postponement.

### After the tribunal or the appeal ends

ARTG2550 requires the customer (or HMRC) to pay or repay tax due as a result of the tribunal's decision on postponed tax. Once an appeal is resolved by any route, ARTG11020 requires the decision maker to amend the record so the correct tax is charged and to **release any postponed or suspended tax**. Additional tax is due and payable **30 days** after the tax payable under the amended decision is notified (ARTG11030), with interest chargeable on unpaid additional tax from that date; where tax is overpaid, ARTG11040 requires prompt repayment with interest. CH146xxx notes repayment interest is **not** paid where an amount is payable as a result of a court or tribunal order that already carries interest (FA 2009 s.102(6)) — for example, successful FTT appeals.

**Hardship** (rule 22, SI 2009/273) applies where the relevant enactment says an appeal may not proceed unless the disputed liability is paid or consent given. ARTG8240 and ARTG8557 both state hardship applications are an **indirect tax** procedure, not directly applicable to income tax Self Assessment. ARTG8557 also notes NIC appellants are not required to pay pending appeal but may pay to limit interest.

## 10. Notifying the appeal to the First-tier Tribunal

### When the appeal may be notified

| Situation | Window | Source |
|---|---|---|
| General (appeal live, no review) | At any time between appealing HMRC's decision and accepting an offer of review, or between appealing and HMRC sending its latest view following a review request | s.49D(1)-(4) TMA 1970 |
| Review offered | Within 30 days of the review offer letter (accept the review instead, or the appeal is treated as settled) | s.49C TMA 1970; ARTG2215 |
| After a completed review | Within 30 days of the conclusion of review letter | s.49G(2), (5)(a) TMA 1970 |
| Review not completed in time | From the day after expiry of the review period to 30 days from the date of HMRC's letter stating the conclusion the review is treated as having reached | ARTG4850 |
| Out of time | Ask the tribunal's permission, explaining the delay | s.49G(3) TMA 1970; rule 20(4) SI 2009/273 |

### Content of the notice of appeal

Rule 20(1)-(3) SI 2009/273 requires the notice of appeal to include the appellant's name and address, the representative's details, an address for documents, details of the decision appealed against, the result sought and the grounds of appeal, and a copy of any written record of the decision and any statement of reasons. ARTG2410 restates the same list and adds a copy of the decision/assessment/review conclusion letter.

The **Clearing House** is HMRC's single contact point with the Tribunals Service for initial notification of appeals; it identifies where in HMRC to send them and does not enter internal correspondence about a case (ARTG8320). The Tribunals Service's **Central Processing Centre** in Birmingham categorises cases and administers Default Paper and Basic cases (ARTG8330).

## 11. FTT procedure: categorisation, directions, hearings, costs

The governing instrument is the **Tribunal Procedure (First-tier Tribunal) (Tax Chamber) Rules 2009, SI 2009/273**. The overriding objective is to deal with cases fairly and justly, and rule 2(4) obliges the parties to help the tribunal further that objective and to co-operate with the tribunal generally.

### Categorisation

On receipt of a notice of appeal, application notice or notice of reference, the tribunal **must** give a direction allocating the case to a category (rule 23(1)):

| Category | Character | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Default Paper | Usually disposed of **without** a hearing | r.23(2)(a) |
| Basic | Usually disposed of after a hearing, with minimal pre-hearing document exchange | r.23(2)(b) |
| Standard | More detailed case management; disposed of after a hearing | r.23(2)(c) |
| Complex | Lengthy or complex evidence/hearing, a complex or important principle or issue, or a large financial sum | r.23(2)(d), r.23(4) |

ARTG8350 gives HMRC's mapping for SA-related appeals:

| Appeal type | Category (ARTG8350) |
|---|---|
| SA and CTSA fixed filing penalties; employer end-of-year late return penalties; construction industry late return penalties; Class 2 NIC late notification penalties; income tax surcharges | Default Paper |
| HMRC applications for daily penalties under s.93(3) TMA 1970 | Default Paper |
| Late filing and late payment (including daily penalties); incorrect return penalties under Sch 24 FA 2007 para 15 — except deliberate action, or where the tax assessment is also appealed; indirect tax reasonable excuse penalties | Basic |
| Cases not categorised as Default Paper, Basic or Complex | Standard |

ARTG8340 notes the tribunal may reallocate a case to a different category at any time, and either party may apply for reallocation; the tribunal retains ultimate discretion over whether a case is decided on papers alone or at a hearing, even where a party requests otherwise.

**Complex cases** may be transferred to the Upper Tribunal (rule 28).

### Timetable by category

| Step | Default Paper | Basic | Standard / Complex |
|---|---|---|---|
| Respondent's statement of case | 42 days after the tribunal sent the notice of appeal (r.25(1)(a); ARTG8370) | — | 60 days after the tribunal sent the notice of appeal (r.25(1)(c); ARTG8390) |
| Appellant's reply | 30 days after HMRC sent its statement of case (r.26(2); ARTG8370) | — | — |
| List of documents | — | — | 42 days after the statement of case, subject to contrary direction (r.27(2); ARTG8390) |
| MP expenses case statement of case | — | — | 28 days (r.25(1)(b)) |

The **statement of case** must state the legal provision under which the appealed decision was made and set out HMRC's position on the issues in dispute (ARTG8370; ARTG8390). ARTG8370 says extension of the 42-day limit is **not automatic** and requires justified reasons; HMRC applies under rule 5(3)(a) or includes a request with the statement of case, with the manager's agreement (ARTG8390). After the appellant's 30-day reply period in a Default Paper case, neither party may submit further evidence or arguments without the tribunal's permission (ARTG8340).

In **Basic** cases, a respondent who becomes aware it intends to raise grounds not previously communicated must notify the appellant as soon as reasonably practicable and in sufficient detail (r.24(3)-(4); ARTG8380 phrases this as "as soon as possible after becoming aware", in enough detail to respond at the hearing).

### Directions and case management

A party applying for a direction must give reasons (r.6(3); ARTG8500). The tribunal writes to all parties and affected persons to notify a direction, unless there is good reason not to (ARTG8500). A decision made by a member of tribunal staff under delegated authority can be required to be reconsidered afresh by a judge on written application **within 14 days** of the tribunal sending notice of that decision (r.4(3)).

### Hearings

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Reasonable notice of the time and place of a hearing to consider disposal | At least **14 days**, unless the parties consent or in urgent/exceptional circumstances (r.31(2); ARTG8480) |
| Upper Tribunal judicial review permission hearings | At least **2 working days** (ARTG8480) |
| "Hearing" | An oral hearing, including by video link, telephone or other instantaneous two-way electronic communication (r.1(3)) |

**Witnesses.** A witness summons requires at least **14 days'** notice of the hearing, or such shorter period as the tribunal directs (r.16(2)(a); ARTG8630). Only persons who could be required to give evidence in a civil court in the same part of the UK can be summoned (ARTG8630). A witness who makes a statement on oath or affirmation knowing it to be false, or not believing it to be true, commits perjury (Perjury Act 1911 ss.1(1), 15(2)).

**Burden of proof.** ARTG8380 states that for **penalties** the burden is on HMRC; for **assessments and return amendments** the burden is on the customer. EM2105-EM2115 (case law summaries) state the taxpayer bears the onus of showing an assessment is excessive.

**New information before the hearing.** ARTG8450 requires the litigator to send new information to the decision maker for consideration, unless it is clear and unambiguous, in which case the litigator may amend the decision directly and notify the customer and the decision maker.

### Withdrawal, strike-out and reinstatement

| Event | Rule |
|---|---|
| Withdrawal | A party may withdraw all or part of its case in writing, or orally at a hearing, at any time before the tribunal makes its decision (r.17; ARTG8460). Withdrawal from the **Upper Tribunal** requires that tribunal's consent (except withdrawing an application for permission to appeal) (ARTG8460) |
| Reinstatement after withdrawal | Application within **28 days** of the date the tribunal received the withdrawal notice, or the date of the hearing at which it was withdrawn (r.17(4); ARTG8460 gives 1 month for the Upper Tribunal) |
| Automatic strike-out | Failure to comply with an "unless order" — a direction stating non-compliance will result in strike-out (r.8(1); ARTG8340) |
| Mandatory strike-out | Tribunal lacks jurisdiction and does not transfer the case under r.5(3)(k)(i) (r.8(2)) |
| Discretionary strike-out | Appellant fails to comply with a direction, fails to co-operate such that the tribunal cannot deal with the case fairly and justly, or the case has no reasonable prospect of success (r.8(3)) |
| Representations | The tribunal must give the appellant an opportunity to make representations before striking out under r.8(2), (3)(b) or (3)(c) — not necessarily under (3)(a) |
| Reinstatement after strike-out | Application received within **28 days** after the date the tribunal sent notification of the striking out (r.8(6); ARTG8340; ARTG8240 gives 1 month for the Upper Tribunal) |
| Barring a respondent | A barred respondent's submissions need not be considered and issues may be summarily determined against them (r.8(8); ARTG8340) |

ARTG8340 adds that where a case is struck out, HMRC returns the case papers to the decision maker to give effect to the prior decision or review conclusion — i.e. the earlier decision stands.

### Costs

The Tax Chamber does not operate general loser-pays costs shifting. Under **rule 10(1)** the tribunal may order costs only:

1. as **wasted costs** under s.29(4) TCEA 2007;
2. where a party has acted **unreasonably** in bringing, defending or conducting the proceedings; or
3. in a **Complex case**, where the taxpayer has not opted out.

| Costs deadline | Period | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Taxpayer's request to be excluded from Complex-case costs liability | Written request within **28 days** of receiving notice that the case was allocated as Complex | r.10(1)(c)(ii), (d) |
| Application for a costs order | Not later than **28 days** after the date the tribunal sends the final decision notice or the withdrawal notice; must include a schedule of costs, sent to the tribunal and the opposing party | r.10(3)-(4) |

Definitions: a **"taxpayer"** for rule 10 purposes is a party liable to pay (or who has paid) the tax, duty, levy or penalty to which the proceedings relate, or part of it, or whose liability to do so is in issue (r.10(8)). A **"legal representative"** is an authorised person for rights of audience or conduct of litigation under the Legal Services Act 2007, an advocate or solicitor in Scotland, or a barrister or solicitor in Northern Ireland (r.11(7)).

### Time and service

Anything required under the Rules must be done **before 5pm** on the specified day; if that day is not a working day, the next working day suffices (r.12(1)-(2)). A **working day** is any day except a Saturday, Sunday, Christmas Day, Good Friday or a bank holiday under s.1 Banking and Financial Dealings Act 1971 (r.12(3)). A party who provides fax or email details must accept delivery by that electronic method unless they opt out (r.13(2)-(3)). A party appointing a representative must send written notice of the representative's name and address to the tribunal and the other parties (r.11(2)).

## 12. Lead cases and related appeals

Where two or more cases before the FTT have similar issues of fact or law ("related cases", ARTG8551), two mechanisms exist — with materially different consequences.

| | **Rule 5(3)(b) and (j) lead case** | **Rule 18 lead case** |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Rule 5(3)(b) lead case; related cases stayed under 5(3)(j) | Rule 18(2)(a) lead case; related cases stayed under 18(2)(b) |
| Binding effect | **Not binding** on stayed cases — persuasive only; stayed customers retain the right to pursue their own case | **Binding in principle** on all related parties, subject to a successful rule 18(4) application |
| Escape route | n/a | Application in writing within **28 days** of the date the tribunal sent a copy of its decision to the parties (r.18(4); ARTG8554) |
| Appeal right for related parties | None against the lead decision | Right of appeal under s.11 TCEA; permission to appeal within **56 days** of the date the rule 18 decision on the related issues is notified (ARTG9060) |
| Source | ARTG8553 | ARTG8554; ARTG9060 |

ARTG8553 notes that for **direct tax** appeals the parties cannot apply for a rule 18 direction if the customer has appealed to HMRC but has not yet notified the appeal to the tribunal, and ARTG8557 says applications for further lead/related case directions cannot be made until the potential lead case(s) have actually notified to the tribunal.

**Normal payment and postponement rules continue to apply to both lead and stayed cases regardless of lead case status** (ARTG8553). Once a rule 18 decision is made, tax is paid or repaid in line with it (ARTG8930; ARTG9060), and ARTG9060 advises decision makers to avoid settling related cases until further appeals are resolved, except where the customer insists.

**Consolidation** under rule 5(3)(b) is a different thing again: related cases are heard together, each determined and binding as if decided alone, with individual rights of appeal (ARTG8552).

## 13. Tribunal applications that are not appeals

Some matters go to the tribunal as **originating applications or references** — "applications not connected with an appeal" (ARTG7510) — rather than as appeals. Rule 21 SI 2009/273 governs their form and any statutory time limit.

| Application | Who | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Application for a direction that HMRC issue a **partial or final closure notice** within a specified period | Taxpayer (or successor, for partnerships) | TMA 1970 s.28A(4) / s.28B(5) |
| Determination of that application | Tribunal **must give the direction applied for** unless satisfied there are reasonable grounds for not issuing the closure notice within a specified period | s.28A(6) / s.28B(7) |
| **Joint referral** of a question arising in connection with the subject matter of an enquiry | HMRC and the taxpayer jointly, in writing, at any time from the date HMRC gave notice of the enquiry until the enquiry is completed | TMA 1970 ss.28ZA-28ZE; ARTG7555 |
| Application for **continuing daily penalties** for failure to make a return | HMRC | s.93(3) TMA 1970 (Default Paper category — ARTG7560) |
| Application for a **tax-related penalty** | HMRC, to the **Upper Tribunal** | para 50(2) Sch 36 FA 2008; ARTG7540 |
| Referral to decide the amount of tax to be **postponed** | Appellant | s.55(3)(b) TMA 1970 |

Notes on closure applications:
- The application may be made **at any time and repeatedly** (EM1976), including immediately on receipt of the opening letter — EM1981 tells enquiry officers to be prepared to contest such an application at that stage.
- The tribunal **cannot** accept the self assessment as returned or amend it; it can only direct the officer to complete the enquiry by a specified date (EM1984).
- A direction for one tax year does not automatically extend to other years unless a separate direction is given (EM1984).
- If HMRC intends to appeal a direction to the Upper Tribunal it must not issue the closure notice but instead seek suspension via case management directions (EM1990).
- Refusal by either party to join a **joint application** carries no right of appeal, and once a joint application is made HMRC cannot issue a closure notice on the referred matter until the tribunal decision, expiry of the appeal period, or withdrawal (ARTG7540). Any appeal against the enquiry conclusion cannot reopen a matter already decided via a joint application.
- There is no right of appeal against s.9A/s.12AC notices themselves, but the closure application route remains open (EM1976).

## 14. Onward appeals and what happens afterwards

The tribunal structure since 1 April 2009 (following the reforms under TCEA 2007) is the First-tier Tribunal, the Upper Tribunal, and then the appellate courts; earlier references to the General or Special Commissioners and the VAT & Duties Tribunal are historical (ARTG000; ARTG2040).

- **FTT → Upper Tribunal**: appeal on a point of law. In a rule 18 related case, permission must be sought within **56 days** of notification of the rule 18 decision (ARTG9060).
- **Upper Tribunal → appellate court**: where the appellate court finds the Upper Tribunal's decision involved an error of law it may set the decision aside, remit with guidance, or remake the decision (ARTG10020).
- **Judicial review**: for FTT's own procedures, applied for directly at the Upper Tribunal; other judicial review cases require an initial application to the High Court or Court of Session, which then considers referral to the Upper Tribunal (ARTG10020).
- **HMRC's own position on continuing enquiries**: EM1984 records HMRC's view that it is entitled to continue enquiries pending a taxpayer's appeal to the Upper Tribunal, on the principle that tribunal decisions stand unless overturned.

Once resolved by any route (agreement, tribunal decision or court decision), the litigator updates records and returns case papers to the decision maker (ARTG11010/11020), who amends the customer's record so the correct tax is charged and releases postponed or suspended tax (ARTG11020). Payment consequences are at §9 above.

## 15. Penalty appeals: reasonable excuse and special reduction

Penalty appeals are appeals like any other, but the substantive test usually turns on reasonable excuse. Points from the notes:

- **Sch 55 FA 2009** applies to income tax and CGT returns for the year ended 5 April 2011 and later; the older TMA 1970 s.93 regime (fixed £100 × 2, daily penalties, over-12-month tax-geared penalty) applies principally to years ended 5 April 2010 and earlier (EM4570; EM4572).
- **Two different excuse tests under the old regime.** For the fixed automatic penalties (s.93(2) and (4)) the taxpayer must show reasonable excuse **throughout the period of default** (s.93(8) & (10)). For daily penalties (s.93(3)) and the over-12-month tax-geared penalty (s.93(5)) the taxpayer must show reasonable excuse for the failure **and** that it was remedied without unreasonable delay (s.118(2) TMA 1970).
- **Fixed vs maximum-amount penalties.** For a **fixed-amount** penalty the amount is set by Parliament and a review officer or tribunal has no discretion as to amount — only to confirm or cancel (EM, on s.93(8)). For a **maximum-amount** penalty the authorised officer sets an appropriate level within a statutory maximum, and a review officer or tribunal can substitute its own judgement (TMA 1970 s.100B(2)(b)).
- **Surcharges (2009-10 and earlier).** On appeal the tribunal can only **confirm or set aside** the surcharge, not revise the percentage, and can set it aside only if the taxpayer had reasonable excuse throughout the whole period of default (s.59C(9)-(10); EM4109). Surcharge appeals must be made within 30 days of the date the surcharge is imposed (s.59C(7)-(10)); late appeals fall under s.49.
- **Special reduction (2010-11 onwards).** SAM guidance says that where there is no reasonable excuse for the failure to file on time, the HMRC officer must consider "special reduction" before disallowing the appeal (CH170100). CH156600 notes the tribunal can only substitute a different level of special reduction where it considers HMRC reached a **"flawed decision"** in applying the special reduction provisions.
- **Late payment penalties under Sch 56 FA 2009.** A penalty is payable within **30 days** beginning on the date the penalty assessment is issued (FA 2009 Sch 56 para 11(2)-(3)), or, where appealed, within 30 days beginning on the date the appeal is finally decided or withdrawn. The assessment is enforceable as if it were a tax assessment.
- **Operational cancellation routes.** SAM10190 describes an "objection" process (from February 2014) letting call handlers cancel a **first** late filing penalty by telephone without a formal written appeal, where narrow conditions are met (CY-1 only; return now filed; no previous year penalty; qualifying reason — illness, a dependant's illness or death, an event outside the taxpayer's control, or a password/activation code issue). It excludes late payment penalties, second late filing penalties, and partnership, military, bankruptcy, deceased and Special Personal Tax cases. This is HMRC operational practice, not a statutory route.
- **Return rejected near the deadline.** SAM10080 records a 21-day re-filing grace period where a return filed by 31 January was rejected or returned as unsatisfactory due to genuine oversight or technical/operational reasons preventing online filing; an appeal against the resulting fixed penalty should be accepted and the penalty cancelled. This applies only in those limited circumstances, not to general late filing.
