# Get help with tax

You can get help if you do not understand something about your tax, for example [tax returns](/self-assessment-tax-returns), allowances and [tax codes](/tax-codes).


You can also get [help and support with Self Assessment](/self-assessment-tax-returns/get-help).


This guide is also available [in Welsh (Cymraeg)](/help-treth).


## Get help from HMRC


For simple queries, you can [contact HM Revenue and Customs](/government/organisations/hm-revenue-customs/contact) (HMRC). You should also contact them in the first instance if you:


- [cannot pay your tax bill on time](/difficulties-paying-hmrc)
- [disagree with a tax decision](/tax-appeals)
- [have additional needs](/dealing-hmrc-additional-needs), for example a disability or you do not speak English


## If you’re on a low income


If HMRC cannot help and you’re on a low income (up to about £380 a week), you may be able to get free professional advice. This includes help with filling in forms and what to do if you owe money to HMRC. Contact:


- [Tax Help for Older People](http://www.taxvol.org.uk/) if you’re over 60 and need help with personal tax
- [TaxAid](http://taxaid.org.uk/) for help with business or personal tax


## Get help from family or friends


You can give permission to a friend or family member (a ‘trusted helper’) to [deal with HMRC on your behalf](/appoint-tax-agent) for things like viewing your Income Tax estimate, talking to HMRC or helping you to fill in forms.


## Hiring a professional


An accountant or tax adviser may be able to help you with your tax. You’ll need to [authorise an accountant or tax adviser](/appoint-tax-agent) to deal with HMRC for you.
